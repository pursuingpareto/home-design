# VS-02 — Scope and acceptance criteria

**Status:** Agreed (2026-11-19). Scope, design values, connections, schema policy, and selectors are recorded as [D051](../../product/decisions/D051-vs02-slice-boundary.md)–[D056](../../product/decisions/D056-dependency-selectors.md). The acceptance criteria are §12.1 and the tasks are §12.2. Sections 2–11 keep the reasoning behind each decision.  
**Roadmap line:** "Richer timber components, connectors, assemblies, dependency propagation" ([roadmap.md](../roadmap.md)).  
**Builds on:** VS-01, complete and verified by `TEST-001`.

## 1. Starting point

VS-01 delivers one parametric house end to end:

- posts and beams generated by `vs01-post-beam-1`;
- 2D and 3D views, a timber schedule, and a gated estimate;
- revisions with preview and undo, a browser client, and CI.

Several things are deliberately absent:

- joists, rafters, braces, purlins, and girders (FR-04);
- connections of any kind;
- walls and assemblies (FR-05);
- per-artifact dependency selectors. Every committing command marks all four artifacts stale (interfaces.md §3, `impacted_artifacts`).

The reference house says the same: connections, assemblies, loads, and foundations are "still open", and must not be invented for engineering claims.

## 2. Candidate themes

The roadmap names four themes. Each is real work with its own prerequisites.

| Theme | What it would add | Value | Main prerequisites and risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Complete frame** | Generated floor joists, common rafters (and ridge support), and knee braces, via a new generation rule version, with parameters such as spacing and section per role | The model looks and schedules like a real timber frame; builders get a fuller takeoff (D009) | Design values for Reference House 001 (spacing, sections, brace geometry) must come from the product owner, never invented. Needs a new rule version and a schema bump with migration of stored projects. Geometry and mesh grow (hundreds of members) |
| **B. Connections** | `Connection` records at member intersections, typed from a deliberately small catalog (for example pegged mortise-and-tenon, and one engineered connector) and shown in 3D, the schedule, and the estimate as counts | FR-04 and D007; the first step toward fabrication info | Which types, and what each records, is open question 4 and needs product input. No engineering checks (FR-07 is VS-04); reference-axis lengths stay "not cut lengths" |
| **C. Assemblies** | Reusable layered `AssemblyDefinition`s (straw bale and stud infill walls first), placed as instances in frame bays, with layer quantities and declared thermal values | FR-05 and D008; homeowner-facing cost and comfort story | Walls need openings (doors and windows) to be useful, plus thermal and moisture data with provenance (open question 6). Climate-specific details must be flagged unverified. This is the largest theme |
| **D. Dependency propagation** | Per-artifact input selectors, so a roof change no longer re-derives the schedule, and `impacted_artifacts` narrows accordingly; optionally an asynchronous worker for heavier derivations | Faster, more honest impact previews (FR-12); groundwork for heavier outputs | Changes a VS-01 contract (`impacted_artifacts` always lists all four), so it needs an approved amendment. The async worker is only worth it once something is slow |

## 3. Scope options

**Option 1: "Complete frame with connections"**, **chosen ([D051](../../product/decisions/D051-vs02-slice-boundary.md))**: themes A and B, plus the selector part of D. Assemblies (C) become the next slice, VS-02b, built on the completed frame.

- **Why this order:**
  - Infill walls and roof assemblies attach to joists, rafters, girts, and sills. Building them on a posts-and-beams frame would mean redoing their placement rules when the frame grows.
  - Connections need the full set of member intersections to be meaningful.
  - Selectors are cheap once more artifacts depend on different inputs, and they make previews honest.
- **Cost:** VS-02 does not yet deliver the straw-bale story from D008. It moves to VS-02b.

**Option 2: thin slice of all four themes.** A few joists and rafters, one connection type, one wall assembly, and selectors.

- Shows progress on every roadmap line.
- Each part is shallow. Assemblies without openings, and connections without a full frame, are likely to need rework.

**Option 3: "Envelope first".** Assemblies (C) plus walls and openings, keeping the posts-and-beams frame.

- Fastest route to the homeowner-facing cost and comfort story.
- Wall placement is defined against a frame that is about to change. Connections and the complete frame wait.

## 4. Cross-cutting concerns (any option)

- **Schema version 2 replaces version 1** ([D055](../../product/decisions/D055-pre-release-schema-replacement.md)):
  - New record kinds and fields bump `model_schema_version` to 2. `vs02-frame-1` replaces `vs01-post-beam-1`.
  - Version 1 snapshots are rejected (`UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION`). There are no readers, upgrades, or migrations while the platform is pre-release.
  - Local databases are recreated.
- **Reference House 001, version 2.** New members and connections need design intent: joist spacing, rafter spacing and sections, brace geometry, connection types. Those values must be supplied or approved by the product owner and recorded in a decision, as D025 was.
- **Engineering boundary unchanged.** Everything stays preliminary (AC-08), with no structural adequacy, code compliance, or load path claims. VS-04 owns analysis.
- **Determinism and golden tests.** The `TEST-001` golden record, the UI fixtures, and the traceability matrix are regenerated for version 2 as reviewed diffs (D055). VS-01's acceptance criteria keep their meaning against the v2 house.
- **Browser.** New member roles need 3D colors and plan symbols, the schedule needs new roles, connections need markers, and new parameters need editors (UI-002 patterns).

## 5. Proposed acceptance criteria (first draft; superseded by §12.1)

- **AC2-01:** New projects are Reference House 001 v2 (version 2, `vs02-frame-1`). Version 1 snapshots are rejected with `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION` (D055).
- **AC2-02:** Joists, rafters, and braces are generated deterministically from stated parameters, with stable IDs under edits. They appear consistently in plan, mesh, and schedule.
- **AC2-03:** Every connection references existing members and a catalog type. It is generated or validated deterministically, and counted in the schedule.
- **AC2-04:** Each artifact declares its inputs. A command's preview lists exactly the artifacts whose inputs it changes, and only those become stale.
- **AC2-05:** VS-01's acceptance criteria still pass against the v2 house, with regenerated fixtures and golden records reviewed as diffs (D055).
- **AC2-06:** Reference House 001 v2 reconciles: member and connection counts match the confirmed design intent, and the schedule and estimate reconcile with the inventory.
- **AC2-07:** Everything new is labeled preliminary. Connection records make no capacity claim.
- **AC2-08:** The traceability matrix, golden record, and property-based tests cover the new rule, connections, and selectors.

## 7. Decision 2 proposal: frame members (approved as D052)

All new members are **generated** by a new rule (working name `vs02-frame-1`) from the grid, levels, roof, and a few new parameters, as in VS-01. User-placed members wait for a later slice. Each role gets a `member_defaults` entry (D039), so sections stay per role (D040).

| Role (new unless noted) | Where | Parameters (new commands) |
|---|---|---|
| `POST`, `BEAM` (existing) | Unchanged from `vs01-post-beam-1`: posts at grid intersections per level; beams along both axes on the Level 1 and eave planes | — |
| `SILL` | Along both axes on the Level 0 plane, like the Level 1 beams. They carry the frame on the foundation, which is still not modeled | None |
| `FLOOR_JOIST` (role already reserved) | Level 1 only, spanning north–south (Y) between the X-axis beams, at a fixed spacing within each bay, with beam lines left free | Spacing (for example 24" on center) |
| `PRINCIPAL_RAFTER` | One pair per X grid line (each bent), from the eave plate to the ridge, on the D048 roof planes | None (follows pitch and the eave plate) |
| `PURLIN` | Along X between adjacent principal rafters, on each slope, at evenly divided positions from eave to ridge | Purlins per slope (for example 2) |
| `KNEE_BRACE` | At post-to-beam junctions on the Level 1 and eave planes, in the plane of each beam, at 45° | Brace leg length (for example 3'), with on/off per plane |

**Why principal rafters and purlins** rather than common rafters on a ridge beam:
- It is the traditional New England heavy-timber roof, so it fits D002–003.
- It follows the grid's bents, so generation keys stay grid-based and stable (D042).
- It keeps member counts in the tens, not hundreds.

The alternative is common rafters at a spacing, plus a ridge beam on king posts. That suits lighter roofs and roof assemblies, but adds more members and a spacing grid of its own.

**Not in this rule:** girts and wall studs (VS-02b, with walls), dormers, hips, and valleys (D031 keeps the plain gable), ceiling joists, and floor framing openings (stairs).

The design values for Reference House 001 v2 (joist spacing, purlins per slope, brace length, and a section for each new role) are decision 3. They are not set here.

## 8. Decision 3 proposal: Reference House 001 v2 design values (approved as D053)

Proposed design intent for the VS-02 fixture, in the spirit of D025. These are **illustrative proportions for a test house, not engineered sizes**. Nothing about them is checked or claimed structurally (AC-08). Every value is for the product owner to approve or change. Existing VS-01 values (footprint, bays, levels, roof, 8×8 posts, 8×12 beams, eastern white pine) are unchanged.

| Parameter | Proposed value | Canonical | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SILL` section | 8×8 full-sawn | 0.2032 × 0.2032 m | Same section as the posts |
| `FLOOR_JOIST` section | 4×8 full-sawn, depth vertical | 0.1016 × 0.2032 m | Timber joists, not dimensional lumber |
| Joist spacing | 24" on center | 0.6096 m | Measured from each bay's west beam line. With 12' bays, that gives 5 joists per bay span |
| `PRINCIPAL_RAFTER` section | 8×10 full-sawn | 0.2032 × 0.2540 m | Depth perpendicular to the roof plane |
| `PURLIN` section | 6×8 full-sawn | 0.1524 × 0.2032 m | |
| Purlins per slope | 2 | — | At 1/3 and 2/3 of the 15' rafter slope (every 5') |
| `KNEE_BRACE` section | 4×6 full-sawn | 0.1016 × 0.1524 m | |
| Brace leg length | 3'-0" | 0.9144 m | Each leg; about a 4'-3" brace at 45° |
| Braces enabled | Level 1 and eave planes | — | One brace at each end of every beam |
| Material, all new roles | Eastern white pine; grade, density, and provenance unknown (`null`) | — | As D025 |

**Resulting inventory: 193 members** (VS-01 had 58):

| Role | Count |
|---|---|
| Posts (unchanged) | 24 |
| Beams (unchanged) | 34 |
| Sills | 17 |
| Floor joists | 30 (3 bays × 2 spans × 5) |

**Open points this proposal does not settle:**
- **Where members sit relative to each other:** for example, whether joists bear on top of beams or frame into them. This affects reference axes only, and is specified in the rule.
- **Which beam ends get braces:** every beam end, or perimeter beams only. Every beam end is proposed, because the approved placement says "at post-to-beam junctions", and the per-plane switch can turn them off.

## 9. Decision 4 proposal: connection catalog (approved as D054)

### 9.1 What a connection is in VS-02

A `Connection` record **joins exactly two members** where one member's end meets the other member. It records:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `connection_type` | Key into the catalog below |
| `member_ids` | The two members: **receiving** (for example the post) and **entering** (for example the beam) |
| `location_m` | Where the entering member's reference axis meets the receiving member's axis |
| `junction_class` | For example `post-beam`; see §9.3 |
| `generation_key` | Derived from the two members' keys, so IDs stay stable under edits (D041, D042) |

**Generated, like members:** connections are produced from the generated frame, one per junction, and checked for conformance (D046). Users do not place or edit connections individually in VS-02. They choose a type **per junction class** (§9.3), as sections are chosen per role (D040).

### 9.2 Catalog: two families, five types

| Type | Family | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | Traditional | Beam, sill, brace, and purlin ends; rafter feet |
| `BRIDLE` | Traditional | Principal rafter pairs at the peak |
| `HOUSED_DOVETAIL` | Traditional | Floor joist ends into beams |
| `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` | Engineered | Concealed steel plate with dowels: the engineered alternative for any timber-to-timber junction except joists |
| `JOIST_HANGER` | Engineered | The engineered alternative for joist ends |

This is the "deliberately limited subset" of FR-04 and D007: one traditional and one engineered option for every junction. Adding types later is a new catalog version, not a schema change.

### 9.3 Junction classes and defaults

Every class defaults to its traditional type. A routine command, for example `SetConnectionType {junction_class, connection_type}`, switches a class to an allowed alternative. Its classification is decided with the commands.

| Junction class | Members | Default | Engineered alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| `post-beam` | Beam end into post (Level 1 and eave planes) | `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |
| `post-sill` | Post foot into sill (Level 0) | `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |
| `brace` | Each brace end, into post or beam | `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |
| `joist-beam` | Each joist end into a beam | `HOUSED_DOVETAIL` | `JOIST_HANGER` |
| `rafter-peak` | Principal rafter pair at the ridge | `BRIDLE` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |
| `rafter-foot` | Principal rafter foot onto the eave beam or post | `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |
| `purlin-rafter` | Each purlin end into a principal rafter | `PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` | `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` |

The exact junction rules, such as which member is receiving at each end, and therefore the connection counts for Reference House 001 v2, are specified with the rule. The counts are then reconciled in tests (AC2-03, AC2-06).

### 9.4 Where connections appear

- **3D and plan:** a small marker at each `location_m`, colored by type, and selectable like members.
- **Schedule:** a connection list, plus counts per type and junction class.
- **Estimate:** one line per connection type, counted "each", priced by a price-book row `connection.<TYPE>`. This extends the price-book unit from meters only to meters or each. Unpriced types stay unpriced (artifacts.md §8.1).

### 9.5 What a connection explicitly does not claim

- Capacity, adequacy, or code compliance (FR-07 is VS-04).
- Peg, dowel, plate, or fastener sizes and positions.
- Machining geometry, or cut lengths. Member lengths stay reference-axis lengths.

Every connection-bearing output carries the preliminary label (AC-08). Each type has a short catalog description that says "representational only".

## 10. Decision 5: schema handling (decided as D055)

The proposal drafted here, with versions 1 and 2 side by side and an opt-in `UpgradeFrameRule` command, was **not adopted**. The platform has no users, so [D055](../../product/decisions/D055-pre-release-schema-replacement.md) applies instead:

- **One schema:** version 2 and `vs02-frame-1` replace version 1 and `vs01-post-beam-1`. Older snapshots are rejected with `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION`.
- **Creation:** `CreateReferenceProject` (arguments still `{}`) creates Reference House 001 v2.
- **Nothing to migrate:** there is no upgrade command, no migration of stored snapshots, and no dual-version code. Local and test databases are recreated.
- **Visible regeneration:** fixtures, golden records, committed schemas, OpenAPI, and test expectations are regenerated as reviewed diffs.
- **Additive version 2 payloads:** new roles, connection markers, connection counts, and "each" estimate lines, documented in artifacts.md. The browser supports version 2 only.
- **Pre-release only:** after the first release, interfaces.md §8 applies in full again.

## 11. Decision 6 proposal: dependency selectors (approved as D056)

**Today** (artifacts.md §2.3, interfaces.md §3):
- every artifact's fingerprint covers the revision ID and the whole snapshot;
- every committing command lists all four artifacts in `impacted_artifacts`;
- every revision derives all four afresh.

**Proposal: per-artifact input selectors, with impact computed by comparing fingerprints.**

1. **Selectors.** Each derivation declares a **selector**: a pure function that extracts, from the canonical snapshot, exactly the records and fields it reads, in canonical form. Its fingerprint hashes that selection, plus `generator_id`, `generator_version`, `model_schema_version`, and any external inputs. The **revision ID is no longer part of the fingerprint.**
2. **Impact is computed, not declared.** A preview computes each artifact's fingerprint for the base and for the candidate. `impacted_artifacts` lists exactly the types whose fingerprints differ. No dependency graph is maintained by hand: the selectors are the graph, and impact is exact by construction.
3. **Reuse across revisions.** After a commit, an artifact whose fingerprint did not change is **reused**: the new revision gets an envelope with its own `source_revision_id`, `head`, and status `CURRENT`, and the same payload, from the cache keyed by `(type, generator_version, fingerprint)`. Only impacted artifacts are derived. Payloads must therefore not embed revision IDs; they already do not.
4. **Proposed selectors for version 2:**

| Artifact | Reads |
|---|---|
| `plan2d` | Footprint, grid, levels, roof, member axes and roles (not sections), connection locations and types |
| `mesh3d` | Everything `plan2d` reads, plus member sections (box sizes) |
| `timber_schedule` | Members (roles, axes, sections, materials), sections, materials, and connections (types and classes) |
| `preliminary_estimate` | The timber schedule's aggregates and connection counts (its upstream payload), plus the price book, as today |

5. **Examples:**
   - Adding an unused section changes no artifact, so the preview lists none.
   - Changing a role's section leaves `plan2d` unaffected.
   - A price-book change still affects only the estimate.
   - Footprint, grid, roof, and story-height changes still affect all four, because they regenerate members.
6. **Contract amendments** (approved with this decision):
   - artifacts.md §2.3: the fingerprint is defined over the selector, not the whole snapshot plus revision ID;
   - interfaces.md §3: `impacted_artifacts` lists exactly the artifacts whose input fingerprints change, possibly none;
   - dependencies.md §2 and §6: declared selectors replace the illustrative edge list, and cache hits across revisions are expected.

   AC-07 is unaffected: every envelope still names its own revision, and stale results are never served as current.
7. **Tests:**
   - For every command type, the property-based sequences assert that an artifact is in `impacted_artifacts` **exactly when** its stored payload for the new revision differs from the base's. This is a selector-completeness check: a selector that misses a field it reads is caught when that field changes and the payload changes unexpectedly.
   - A determinism test asserts that a reused payload equals a fresh derivation.

**Alternative:** a static, field-level dependency graph (dependencies.md §2 style), declared separately from the derivations. It is more machinery, can drift from what the code actually reads, and gains nothing over comparing fingerprints at VS-02's scale.

**Not in VS-02:** asynchronous workers (D051). Derivation stays synchronous after commit and lazy on read.

## 12. Acceptance criteria and task breakdown (approved)

### 12.1 VS-02 acceptance criteria (revised for D051–D056; these replace §5)

- **AC2-01: version 2 only.** New projects are Reference House 001 v2 (version 2, `vs02-frame-1`). Version 1 snapshots are rejected with `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION` (D055).
- **AC2-02: complete frame.**
  - Sills, floor joists, principal rafters, purlins, and knee braces are generated deterministically from the D052 rules and D053 parameters: 193 members for the reference house.
  - IDs are stable under edits, including the new parameter commands (joist spacing, purlins per slope, braces).
  - Plan, mesh, and schedule agree.
- **AC2-03: connections.**
  - Connections are generated per D054: two members each, a type per junction class, and stable IDs.
  - Switching a class's type is a validated command.
  - Counts reconcile with the frame.
- **AC2-04: exact impact.** `impacted_artifacts` lists an artifact if and only if its payload changes. Unchanged artifacts are reused for the new revision (D056).
- **AC2-05: VS-01 still holds.** AC-01 to AC-09 pass against the v2 house. Regenerated fixtures and golden records are reviewed as diffs (D055).
- **AC2-06: the reference house reconciles.**
  - 193 members, and the connection count the rule specifies.
  - The schedule reconciles members and connections.
  - The estimate prices timber per meter and connections per piece. Unpriced items are never shown as 0.
- **AC2-07: still preliminary.** Every output is labeled preliminary. Connection records make no capacity claim, and lengths stay reference-axis lengths.
- **AC2-08: browser.**
  - New members and connection markers appear in 2D, 3D, and the schedule.
  - The frame parameters and connection types are editable through the existing preview and apply flows.
  - The price-book form handles "each" rows.
- **AC2-09: traceability.** The traceability matrix covers AC2-01 to AC2-08. The golden record, property-based sequences (including the if-and-only-if impact check), and CI run them.
