### 4.2 Walls

One row per `Wall`, sorted by `generation_key` (code-unit order):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `level_id`, `level_index` | The wall's level, and its `index` (D070's "story") |
| `elevation_side` | `south`, `north`, `west`, or `east`: walls along X on the first or last Y line, and walls along Y on the first or last X line (+Y is north, D026; footprints are unrotated, D031) |
| `assembly_definition_id`, `assembly_label` | Its definition (D068: the project default) |
| `length_m` | `reference_length(start_m, end_m)`, the schedule's function (artifacts.md §7) |
| `height_m` | The record's `height_m` |
| `gross_area_m2` | `length_m * height_m` |
| `opening_area_m2` | The sum of its openings' `area_m2`, in the `openings` row order (§4.3); `0` with none |
| `net_area_m2` | `gross_area_m2 - opening_area_m2` |
| `framing_face_area_m2` | §4.4 |
| `framing_fraction` | `framing_face_area_m2 / net_area_m2` (D071's `f`) |
| `u_value_w_m2_k`, `r_value_m2_k_w`, `ua_w_k` | The wall's parallel-path figures (§4.8), or `null` |

Every value is evaluated exactly as written, in IEEE-754 doubles, as the schedule's lengths are.

### 4.3 Openings

One row per `Opening`, sorted by its wall's `generation_key`, then by opening ID.

- **Fields:** D070's "kind, rough-opening size, wall, and whether it has jacks", plus the wall's key for reading and `area_m2 = width_m * height_m`.
- **`has_jacks`** is `true` exactly when a `JACK` piece names the opening. It is read from the generated pieces, so the schedule never restates the threshold rule. Straw bale openings are always `false`.
- **No position:** no `offset_m` or `sill_height_m`. Positions belong to the views, as for connections (ART-002 §6.1), so moving a window changes the plan and the mesh but not the schedule, unless its framing changes.

### 4.4 Wall framing rows and face areas

- **One row per `WallFramingPiece`**, sorted by `generation_key`, so each wall's pieces follow one another. The fields mirror the timber schedule's member rows (artifacts.md §7), plus `wall_id` (the wall that lists it) and `opening_id`. `length_m` is the reference-axis length, computed by `reference_length`. There is no position.
- **A piece's face** is the rectangle it covers in its wall's plane: its in-plane dimension (D076: the section's `actual_width_m`, or `actual_depth_m` for a header) centered on its axis, times its axis.
- **`framing_face_area_m2`**, per wall, counts girt crossings once (D071; ASM-001 §5.6):
  1. the sum, in piece key order, of `in_plane * length_m` over the wall's pieces;
  2. minus, in the order (girt piece key, vertical piece key), the overlap area of each girt piece's face with each vertical piece's face (studs, kings, jacks, cripples, and buck jambs), where both the overlap's width and height exceed ε. Each overlap is `(min(hi) − max(lo))` along the wall times the same up the wall, evaluated as written.

  ASM-001 §5.6 guarantees that no other two faces overlap. A test checks it on both families.
- **Reference House 001 v3**, measured with a prototype of this rule:

| | Straw bale (fixture) | Stud infill |
|---|---|---|
| Framing face area | 3.484832 m² | 20.156573 m² (after 0.110322 m² of girt crossings) |
| Whole-house fraction (face area / net area) | 1.86% | 10.76% |
| Per wall | 2.31% (5'-0" windows), 2.07% (door), 1.68% (Level 0, 3'-0" windows), 1.89% (Level 1) | 13.05%, 10.43%, 10.30%, 10.68% |

  The fractions exclude the heavy-timber frame in the wall plane (posts, sills, beams, braces), and there are no plates. That is D071's method, and its note says so. It is why the stud infill fraction is about half a typical published framing factor for 2×6 walls at 24".

### 4.5 Definitions

One entry per assembly definition **used by at least one wall**, sorted by label (one in VS-02b, D068). Each holds what a reader of the figures needs:

- `assembly_definition_id`, `label`, `family`, `installation_note`, `unverified_climate_details` (D081's text, in its order);
- `total_thickness_m`: the layers' thicknesses summed in order;
- `layers`, exterior to interior: `name`, `function`, `material_id`, `material_name`, `thickness_m`, and the three declared values exactly as stored (each `{value, source, date, note}` or `null`, D075);
- for the framing layer, `framing`: its `type`, `framing_material_id`, `framing_material_name`, `framing_thermal_conductivity_w_m_k` (as stored), and its sections by label and parameters (`STUD`: `stud_section`, `stud_spacing_m`, `girt_count`, `girt_section`, `header_section`, `jack_threshold_width_m`; `BUCK`: `buck_section`, `girt_count`).

Unused definitions are not listed, as unused sections and materials are in no selection (D061). The comparison with the other family is made by switching (`SetWallAssembly`, with its preview).

### 4.6 Aggregates and the framing-layer fill

- **`definition_aggregates`:** per definition in use: wall and opening counts, and gross, opening, and net area, summed over the wall rows in row order.
- **`layer_aggregates`:** per definition in use and layer, in build-up order (exterior to interior), with `function`, `material_name`, `thickness_m`, `area_m2`, and `volume_m3 = area_m2 * thickness_m`:
  - **non-framing layers:** `area_m2` is the sum of the walls' net areas (D070);
  - **the framing layer's fill** (§12 item 1): `area_m2` is the sum over walls of `net_area_m2 - framing_face_area_m2`, the wall area the fill (bales or cellulose) occupies between the framing pieces. So its `volume_m3` is the fill volume net of framing. With the recommendation, the reference house has 183.807697 m² of bales (84.0369 m³) and, with stud infill, 167.135956 m² of cellulose (23.3489 m³).

### 4.7 Assumptions

`assumptions` lists, in this order, what the quantities do not count or model (AC2b-09; D064, D066, D069, D070):

| Code | Message |
|---|---|
| `WALLS_ONLY` | Walls only: perimeter infill walls between the frame's posts. Gable-end walls, roof, and floor assemblies are not included. |
| `REFERENCE_AREA` | Wall areas are reference areas: bay length between grid stations times story height between datums, less rough openings. Member faces, knee braces, and the framing around openings are not deducted. |
| `REFERENCE_AXIS_LENGTH` | Wall framing lengths are reference-axis distances, not cut lengths. |
| `FILL_NET_OF_FRAMING` | The framing layer's fill (bales or cellulose) is quantified as each wall's net area less its framing's face area. Bale counts are not given. |
| `NO_PLATES_OR_NOTCHES` | No bottom or top plates are generated, and the let-in notches where girts cross studs are not modeled. |
| `FASTENINGS_NOT_COUNTED` | Wall framing fastenings and attachments (stud ends, girt to post, headers, and opening framing) are not modeled or counted. |
| `JACK_THRESHOLD_DECLARED` | Jack studs follow the definition's declared jack threshold width, which is not derived from any building code. |
| `OPENINGS_ARE_VOIDS` | Doors and windows are rough openings: no unit, frame, glazing, or hardware is counted, and the estimate does not price them. |
| `FIT_TO_AXES_ONLY` | Openings are checked for fit against member axes and datums only; clearance to member faces is not checked. |

`FILL_NET_OF_FRAMING` takes the wording of §12 item 1's outcome. The thermal method's labels are not here but in `thermal.method.notes` (§4.8), so that the estimate, which copies `assumptions` (§7.3), carries no thermal text.

### 4.8 Thermal figures (D071, `parallel-path-1`)

**Layer resistances,** per definition in use, each evaluated as written:

- a non-framing layer: `thickness_m / k` with a declared conductivity `k`, or the declared resistance's value; **unknown** when neither is declared;
- the framing layer through the fill: `thickness_m / k_fill`, from the layer's own conductivity; through the framing: `thickness_m / k_framing`, from `framing.framing_thermal_conductivity_w_m_k`. Each is unknown when its conductivity is `null`.

**Path resistances:** `R_fill` is the sum of every layer's resistance, exterior to interior, with the framing layer at its fill value; `R_framing` the same with the framing layer at its framing value. **No surface air films** are added (§12 item 3).

**Figures:**

| Figure | Where | Formula | Known when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Framing fraction `f` | Each wall row | §4.4 | Always (from the generated framing) |
| Center of cavity | Each definition | `r = R_fill`, `u = 1 / R_fill` | Every non-framing layer and the fill have a value |
| Framing path | Each definition | `r = R_framing`, `u = 1 / R_framing` | Every non-framing layer and the framing material have a value |
| Parallel path, per wall | Each wall row | `U = f / R_framing + (1 - f) / R_fill`; `r_value = 1 / U`; `ua = U * net_area_m2` | Both paths are known |
| Area-weighted, per definition and for all walls | `thermal.definitions[]`, `thermal.all_walls` | `ua` summed over the walls in row order; `u = ua / net area`; `r = 1 / u` | Every wall in the group has its `U` |

- **Gating is by input, not by wall:** a wall with no framing pieces (`f = 0`) still needs `R_framing`. That keeps one rule, and a straw bale wall without openings does not show a figure that its neighbors lack.
- **Missing inputs are named:** each unknown figure lists `missing_inputs`, entries `{assembly_label, layer, fields}`, where `fields` names what would supply it: `["thermal_conductivity_w_m_k", "thermal_resistance_m2_k_w"]` (either) for a non-framing layer, `["thermal_conductivity_w_m_k"]` for the fill, and `["framing.framing_thermal_conductivity_w_m_k"]` for the framing material. Per wall, the row's figures are `null`, and the definition entry names the missing inputs once.

## 5. `plan2d`

### 5.1 Where layers sit through the wall (§12 item 2)

A wall's reference plane is its grid line (D065). The payloads need each layer's position through the wall:

- **The framing layer is centered on the reference plane,** so every framing piece, whose axis lies in that plane (ASM-001), sits inside its layer;
- the layers before it in the build-up are stacked toward the **exterior**, and those after it toward the interior. The exterior is away from the footprint: south for the first Y line, and so on (`elevation_side`, §4.2).

Each layer then has a signed interval `[inner_m, outer_m]` along the wall's outward normal, evaluated from the framing layer outward:

```text
framing layer k:  outer = thickness_k / 2;  inner = -(thickness_k / 2)
exterior layers j = k-1 … 0:   inner_j = outer_(j+1);   outer_j = inner_j + thickness_j
interior layers j = k+1 … n-1: outer_j = inner_(j-1);   inner_j = outer_j - thickness_j
exterior_m = outer_0;   interior_m = -inner_(n-1)
```

| Definition | Exterior of the plane | Interior | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `straw-bale` | 10½" (0.2667 m) | 10" (0.254 m) | 20½" |
| `stud-infill` | 4" (0.1016 m) | 3¼" (0.08255 m) | 7¼" |

### 5.2 Walls and openings

The `level-0` and `level-1` planes gain `walls`; every plane has the array, empty on `eave` and `roof`. Each wall is on its own level's plane:

```json
{"wall_id": "…", "generation_key": "wall/axis-x/y0/x0-x1/level-0",
 "start_xy_m": [0.0, 0.0], "end_xy_m": [3.6576, 0.0], "outward_xy": [0.0, -1.0],
 "exterior_m": 0.2667, "interior_m": 0.254,
 "openings": [{"opening_id": "…", "opening_kind": "WINDOW", "start_xy_m": [1.0668, 0.0], "end_xy_m": [2.5908, 0.0]}]}
```

- **The outline** is the band from `start_xy_m` to `end_xy_m`, `exterior_m` outward and `interior_m` inward (D070: "an outline of its total thickness").
- **Gaps at openings:** each opening is a gap through the full thickness at its **rough opening** along the reference line, at `start + offset_m` and `start + (offset_m + width_m)` along the wall, evaluated as written. Doors and windows alike are gaps, as a plan cut shows them. Openings are sorted by `offset_m`, then ID.
- **Sorted** by `generation_key`.

### 5.3 Wall framing

The same planes gain `wall_framing`, one entry per piece on its wall's plane, sorted by the piece's `generation_key`:

- vertical pieces (`STUD`, `KING`, `JACK`, `CRIPPLE`, `BUCK_JAMB`): `{piece_id, piece_kind, xy_m}`, the plan projection of `start_m`, as for posts;
- horizontal pieces (`GIRT`, `HEADER`, `ROUGH_SILL`, `BUCK_HEAD`, `BUCK_SILL`): `{piece_id, piece_kind, start_xy_m, end_xy_m}`.

No section and, under §12 item 5, no `generation_key`: clients join on `piece_id` with the assembly schedule for keys and sections, as they join members for sections (ART-002 §4.3).

### 5.4 The switches

`walls` (the build-ups, with their openings) and `wall_framing` are separate arrays, so the browser's "Wall layers" and "Wall framing" switches (D066) each hide one array, as the Connections switch hides `connections` (D062). Nothing else in the payload depends on them.

### 5.5 Unchanged

Members, connections, `footprint`, `roof_plan`, `dimensions` (still exactly two), and plane order.

## 6. `mesh3d`

`generator_version` moves to `"3"`.

### 6.1 Walls

A top-level `walls` array, sorted by `generation_key`, one display primitive per wall:

```json
{"wall_id": "…", "generation_key": "wall/axis-x/y0/x0-x1/level-0", "primitive": "layered_wall",
 "base_start_m": [0.0, 0.0, 0.0], "base_end_m": [3.6576, 0.0, 0.0], "height_m": 3.048, "outward_xy": [0.0, -1.0],
 "layers": [{"name": "lime-plaster", "function": "FINISH", "inner_m": 0.2286, "outer_m": 0.2667},
            {"name": "bales", "function": "FRAMING", "inner_m": -0.2286, "outer_m": 0.2286},
            {"name": "clay-plaster", "function": "FINISH", "inner_m": -0.254, "outer_m": -0.2286}],
 "voids": [{"opening_id": "…", "opening_kind": "WINDOW", "u_m": [1.0668, 2.5908], "v_m": [0.9144, 2.1336]}]}
```

- **A `layered_wall`** is one box per layer: along the wall from `base_start_m` to `base_end_m`, up by `height_m`, and through the wall from `inner_m` to `outer_m` along `outward_xy` (§5.1). The framing layer's box is its fill (D071), so with wall layers shown it hides the framing inside it, which is the point of D066's two switches.
- **Voids** are the rough openings, in wall-local coordinates (`u` along the wall from its start, `v` up from its base), through every layer (D070: "boxes with rectangular voids"). Sorted by `u_m[0]`, then ID.
- **The client tessellates,** as for member boxes. It is a display primitive, not a solid model.

### 6.2 Wall framing

A top-level `wall_framing` array, one box per piece, sorted by the piece's `generation_key`:

```json
{"piece_id": "…", "piece_kind": "KING", "wall_id": "…",
 "axis_start_m": [1.35255, 0.0, 0.0], "axis_end_m": [1.35255, 0.0, 3.048],
 "section_in_plane_m": 0.0381, "section_through_wall_m": 0.1397}
```

- **Orientation** (a rule of its own, because a stud's section is not oriented like a member's): `section_through_wall_m` lies along its wall's `outward_xy`; `section_in_plane_m` lies in the wall's plane, perpendicular to the axis. So a stud's 1½" runs along the wall and a girt's 1½" is vertical (D076).
- **Dimensions:** in-plane is the section's `actual_width_m`, through-wall its `actual_depth_m`, and the header the reverse (D076: its depth is vertical, in the plane). The header is one piece, 1½" through the wall (D066: plies are not counted).
- **No `generation_key` and no `primitive` field** (§12 item 5): every entry is an oriented box, and keys are in the assembly schedule.

### 6.3 Unchanged

`members`, `connections`, `envelope`, and their rules (artifacts.md §6).

## 7. `preliminary_estimate` and price books

`generator_version` moves to `"3"`.

### 7.1 Line items

Two new `kind`s join the discriminated union (ART-002 §7.1):

```json
{"kind": "assembly", "key": "assembly.straw-bale.lime-plaster", "assembly_label": "straw-bale", "layer": "lime-plaster",
 "layer_function": "FINISH", "material_name": "Lime plaster",

```

- **`assembly` lines:** one per `layer_aggregates` row (D070: "one line per definition and non-framing layer"), and, under §12 item 1, one for the framing layer's fill. `quantity_value` is the row's `area_m2`. - **`wallframing` lines:** one per `framing_aggregates` row, key `wallframing.<piece_kind>.<nominal_section>`, with `quantity_value` the total length in meters and `count` beside it (D070). Keys are matched whole, so the dot in `1.5x18` is harmless (ASM-001 §13 item 9).
- **Amounts:** `unit_price_minor × quantity_value`, exact, rounded half-even, the same function as timber lines (artifacts.md §8.1).
- **Order:** by key, code-unit order: `assembly.`, then `connection.`, `timber.`, `wallframing.`.
- **Coverage:** the §8.1 rule over every line. A book that prices only the frame becomes `partial` once wall lines exist, with `grand_total_minor: null` and `COST_INPUTS_MISSING`. Unpriced lines are never 0 (AC2b-05).
- **Reference House 001 v3 (straw bale):** 16 lines: `assembly.straw-bale.bales`, `.clay-plaster`, and `.lime-plaster`; the three connection lines; the seven timber lines; and `wallframing.BUCK_HEAD.1.5x18`, `BUCK_JAMB`, and `BUCK_SILL`. With stud infill: 4 assembly lines and 7 wallframing lines, 21 in all.

### 7.2 Price books

- `PriceRow.unit` gains **`m2`** (price per square meter).
- **Units by family** (`UNIT_BY_FAMILY`): `assembly.` needs `m2` and `wallframing.` needs `m`, beside `timber.` → `m` and `connection.` → `each`. A mismatch is `PRICE_UNIT_MISMATCH` at `rows[k].unit`, with `related_paths` `["rows[k].key"]`, as today (D070).
- **Unused rows:** a row for the other family's lines (for example `wallframing.STUD.2x6` while every wall is straw bale) is named in `PRICE_ROWS_UNUSED`, not rejected. `SetWallAssembly` therefore turns one family's priced lines into unused rows and the other's into unpriced lines, and coverage shows it, as `SetConnectionType` does.

### 7.3 Inputs and assumptions

- **The estimate depends on both schedules:** `depends_on = ("timber_schedule", "assembly_schedule")`. If either is not `CURRENT`, the estimate is `FAILED` with `UPSTREAM_NOT_CURRENT`, as today.
- **`assumptions`** is the timber schedule's list followed by the assembly schedule's, copied, so the reader of a total sees that openings are not priced and fastenings not counted (D070).

## 12. Proposals for approval

**Your judgment** (one at a time, in this order; approved items become decisions from **D082**, which is free on `main` and on every remote branch, checked 2026-11-22):

1. **The framing layer's fill quantity** (§4.6; MODEL-006 §13, "Noted for later task files"). **Approved (2026-11-22): net of framing, as recommended; recorded as [D082](../../product/decisions/D082-vs02b-framing-layer-fill-quantity.md).** D070 quantifies the framing layer "by its pieces", and D071 makes the bales and the cellulose that layer's fill. Read together, the straw and the cellulose get no area, no volume, and no estimate line, although they are the main material of each wall. *Recommendation:* the fill gets a `layer_aggregates` row and an `assembly.<label>.<layer>` line, priced per m² like the other layers, with its **area net of framing**: each wall's net area less its framing face area (§4.4), and its volume that area times the layer's thickness. Reference house: 183.81 m² and 84.04 m³ of bales (1.9% less than the net area); with stud infill, 167.14 m² and 23.35 m³ of cellulose (10.8% less). The line is labeled by its material, because the stud infill layer is named `framing`. *Alternatives:*
   - **the full net area,** like every other layer: simpler, and one area for every line of a definition, but it overstates the fill by the framing fraction;
   - **no fill quantity,** with an assumption line saying the fill is not quantified: D070 read literally, but the bales are the straw bale wall's main cost.
2. **Where layers sit through the wall** (§5.1). **Approved (2026-11-22): the framing layer centered, as recommended; recorded as [D083](../../product/decisions/D083-vs02b-layer-placement.md).** D065 places the build-up "symmetrically about" the grid line "unless the approved build-ups say otherwise", and ASM-001 puts every framing piece's axis on that line. The build-ups are not symmetric: centering the whole build-up would put the straw bale wall's bucks ¼" off the center of the bale layer, and the stud infill wall's studs ⅜" off the center of their cavity. So in 3D the framing would poke out of its own layer. *Recommendation:* **center the framing layer on the reference plane,** with the other layers stacked outward and inward from it in build-up order. Straw bale then extends 10½" outside the grid line and 10" inside; stud infill 4" and 3¼". *Alternative:* **center the whole build-up** (10¼" each side for straw bale), which reads D065 literally, but then the views must offset every framing piece from its model axis, or draw it outside its layer.
3. **Surface air films in `parallel-path-1`** (§4.8). **Approved (2026-11-22): no air films, as recommended; recorded as [D084](../../product/decisions/D084-vs02b-no-surface-air-films.md).** D071 sums "every layer's resistance", and the model has no surface resistances. Published parallel-path calculations usually add inside and outside air-film resistances, which raise R and lower U by a few percent for these build-ups. Adding them needs sourced values, which open question 6 leaves open. *Recommendation:* **no air films** in `parallel-path-1`; the method note says "Surface air films are not included", so the figure is conservative (U slightly high) and every number in it comes from the definition. A later method version can add films with sourced values. *Alternative:* **fixed film values in the method,** with their source named in the note: closer to published practice, but it puts the first property values into code rather than into the model with provenance (D071, D075).
4. **The browser interim** (§10). **Approved (2026-11-22): the full interim, as recommended; recorded as [D085](../../product/decisions/D085-vs02b-browser-interim.md).** *Recommendation:* the estimate table and the price-book form handle `assembly.` and `wallframing.` lines and `m2` rows here, and the workspace knows the fifth type. AC2b-10's price-book bullet moves into this task, as D060 did for "each" rows. Walls are not drawn until `UI-004`. *Alternative:* **only stop the breakage:** labels for the new lines and the type, with `m2` rows read-only in the form until `UI-004`. That is less work here, but then the API can set wall prices that the browser cannot edit.
5. **No keys on wall framing in the views** (§5.3, §6.2, §9.4). **Approved (2026-11-22): IDs only, as recommended; recorded as [D086](../../product/decisions/D086-vs02b-wall-framing-ids-in-views.md).** With stud infill, the reference house's mesh would be about 255,500 bytes with the member pattern (a `generation_key` and `primitive` on every box): 97% of D063's 256 KiB ceiling. *Recommendation:* wall framing entries in `plan2d` and `mesh3d` carry `piece_id` and no key, and mesh boxes carry no `primitive` field. ## 17. Implementation notes (2026-11-22)

### Where it lives

- **`assemblies/thermal.py`:** `parallel-path-1` as a pure module (`path_resistances`, `wall_figures`, `thermal_section`), as §15 suggests.

### Implementation choices (within the approved text)

1. **Face rectangles use global coordinates.** §15 suggests wall-local `(u, v)`. The implementation uses the global coordinate along the wall's axis and global `z`, which is the same rectangle without a subtraction (`u = x − A`) that could round. The overlap rule and the oracle use the same coordinates, so they agree bit for bit.
2. **`elevation_side` from the key.** The selector reads the wall's axis and line ID from its D080 key and compares the line ID with the grid's first and last lines (§4.2's wording). The oracle derives the side independently, from the wall's position against the footprint's center line.
3. **Opening positions along a wall** are `start + offset` on the wall's along-axis coordinate (footprints are unrotated, D031), evaluated as §5.2 writes it.
4. **`CLIMATE_DETAILS_UNVERIFIED` on every envelope.** §4.9 says every `assembly_schedule` envelope carries it. It is added by the envelope builder with `PRELIMINARY_UNVERIFIED` (`STANDING_WARNINGS`), so FAILED and MISSING envelopes carry it too, not only stored rows.
5. **Thermal output details.** `missing_inputs` lists entries in build-up order. The framing layer's fill entry comes before its framing entry, and the parallel-path union is the two lists merged in that order. A known figure has `missing_inputs: []`. A known resistance takes the conductivity first; MODEL-006 rejects a layer that declares both (D075).
6. **Plan framing entries are a union discriminated by `piece_kind`,** so OpenAPI and `schema.d.ts` narrow vertical and horizontal pieces.
7. **Selections order openings by wall key, then ID.** The view builders then sort each wall's openings by offset, then ID (§5.2, §6.1).
8. **`build_estimate(timber, assemblies, book)`.** The builder takes both schedules' lists. Its callers (the derivation, the invariants, and the budget and estimate tests) pass both.
