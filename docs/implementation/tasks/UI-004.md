## 1. Objective and user-visible behavior

Show Reference House 001 v3's walls, openings, and wall framing in the browser, and make the four wall commands editable there. This closes AC2b-10's first two bullets (its price-book bullet shipped with ART-003, [D085](../../product/decisions/D085-vs02b-browser-interim.md)) and the browser parts of [D066](../../product/decisions/D066-vs02b-wall-framing.md) (the two switches), [D068](../../product/decisions/D068-vs02b-wall-assembly-choice.md) (the assembly choice), [D069](../../product/decisions/D069-vs02b-openings.md) (the openings), and [D070](../../product/decisions/D070-vs02b-quantities-and-outputs.md)–[D071](../../product/decisions/D071-vs02b-thermal-moisture-values.md) (the assembly schedule and its figures).

**User-visible after this task:**

- **Plan:** on the Level 0 and Level 1 planes, each wall is drawn as a band of its build-up's thickness, placed as D083 says, with a gap at each rough opening; doors and windows are marked in their gaps; wall framing is drawn in plan (§4).
- **3D:** each wall is drawn as its layers, with rectangular voids at the rough openings, and each wall framing piece as a box of its section (§5).
- **Two switches,** "Wall layers" and "Wall framing", beside "Connections", in both views (§6, D066).
- **Selection:** walls, openings, and wall framing pieces can be selected in the plan, the 3D view, or the assembly schedule, and are highlighted in all three. Selecting an opening also highlights its framing (§7).
- **Walls tab:** the wall assembly for the whole house (`SetWallAssembly`, through preview and confirmation), and the openings: add, change, or remove a door or window (`AddOpening`, `SetOpening`, `RemoveOpening`, applied directly with Undo, as routine edits are) (§8; item 1).
- **Confirmation dialog:** wall and wall framing counts beside the member and connection counts, the definition's name instead of its ID, and the openings that a frame edit would remove, listed by name (§9; item 2).
- **Assembly schedule:** a new panel under the timber schedule: the definition in use with its layers, declared values and their provenance, its framing parameters, its installation note, and its unverified climate details under the envelope's warning; the thermal figures with their method and notes, or "Not computed" with the missing inputs; the walls, openings, framing takeoff, layer quantities, the piece list, and the assumptions (§10; item 3 sets the units of the thermal figures).

Nothing here claims structural, fire, energy-code, or moisture performance (AC2b-06, AC2b-09). Every figure is shown with its method, its "declared values, not verified" note, and the unverified-climate warning, and an unknown figure is never shown as 0.

## 4. Walls, openings, and wall framing in the plan (items 4–5)

The `level-0` and `level-1` planes carry `walls` and `wall_framing` (artifacts.md §5, "VS-02b"); `eave` and `roof` carry empty arrays.

### 4.1 Walls and openings (item 4)

- **The band:** each wall is a filled rectangle from `start_xy_m` to `end_xy_m`, `exterior_m` along `outward_xy` and `interior_m` against it, exactly as served (D083: 10½" out and 10" in for straw bale; 4" and 3¼" for stud infill). The client computes the four corners from those values and nothing else.
- **Gaps:** the band is drawn as pieces between its openings: from the wall's start to the first opening's `start_xy_m`, between openings, and from the last opening's `end_xy_m` to the wall's end. Openings are in served order.
- **Openings in their gaps:** each opening is a rectangle across the full thickness at its gap, outlined, so it can be seen and clicked. A window has a single line along the reference line (a conventional glazing mark), a door none; both have the opening kind in a data attribute. Neither says anything about a unit, frame, or swing (D069: voids, not products).
- **Drawing order:** wall bands first, under the members; then members as today; then wall framing (§4.2); then connection glyphs. Members stay visible: posts and beam lines lie on the grid lines inside the bands.
- **Tooltip:** the wall's label (§12) and its generation key; an opening's label and its rough-opening size.
- **Reference House 001 v3:** 10 walls and 10 openings on each of `level-0` (1 door, 9 windows) and `level-1` (10 windows).

### 4.2 Wall framing (item 5)

- **Symbols, not sections:** the plan payload has no sections (D086), so vertical pieces (`STUD`, `KING`, `JACK`, `CRIPPLE`, `BUCK_JAMB`) are small squares of fixed size at `xy_m`, as posts are (`POST_MARK_M`), and horizontal pieces (`GIRT`, `HEADER`, `ROUGH_SILL`, `BUCK_HEAD`, `BUCK_SILL`) are thin lines from `start_xy_m` to `end_xy_m`. The code says the mark size is a symbol where the constant is defined.
- **Coincident pieces share one mark** (D062's rule, applied to framing): a plan cut projects pieces at different heights onto one point or line. A header and its rough sill have the same projection, so do a buck head and its buck sill, and so do the upper and lower cripples of one layout position. Entries are grouped by exact coordinate equality (`xy_m`, or the pair `start_xy_m`, `end_xy_m`), per plane. A mark for one piece behaves as a single piece; a mark for several shows no count, lists every piece in its tooltip, and selects all of them. Measured on Reference House 001 v3:

| Plane | Straw bale: pieces | Marks | Stud infill: pieces | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `level-0` | 39 (20 vertical, 19 horizontal) | 30 (9 pairs) | 126 (87, 39) | 106 (20 pairs) |
| `level-1` | 40 (20, 20) | 30 (10 pairs) | 120 (80, 40) | 100 (20 pairs) |

- **Colors:** three framing colors, by group (§12): layout framing (studs, girts), opening framing (kings, jacks, headers, rough sills, cripples), and bucks. The kind is in the tooltip, from the assembly schedule's row joined on `piece_id` (with its nominal section), or the kind alone when that schedule is not `CURRENT` for the views' revision.
- **Unchanged:** members, connections, the footprint, eave ring, ridge, dimensions, and plane tabs.

## 5. Walls, openings, and wall framing in 3D (item 6)

### 5.1 Wall layers

- **One opaque solid per layer** of each `layered_wall` (artifacts.md §6): along the wall from `base_start_m` to `base_end_m`, up `height_m`, and from `inner_m` to `outer_m` along `outward_xy`, as served. The framing layer's solid is its fill, so with wall layers shown the framing inside it is hidden; that is what D066's two switches are for.
- **Voids:** each layer is tessellated around the wall's `voids` (in wall-local `u` and `v`), so every rough opening is a hole through every layer. The client tessellates; it computes no other geometry (UI-001 §6).
- **Colors by layer function** (`FINISH`, `SHEATHING`, `INSULATION`, and the `FRAMING` layer's fill), from the palette (§12). The layer's name and material are in the tooltip, from the payload's `name` and the assembly schedule's definition when it is `CURRENT`.
- **Opening outlines:** each void gets a thin outline on the wall's exterior face and an invisible pick surface across it, so an opening can be hovered and selected though it is a hole. The outline is a symbol, not a frame or glazing (D069).
- **Members are unchanged** and still drawn. With wall layers on, the perimeter posts, sills, and beams lie inside the layers (D065: in-plane infill), so they are hidden and are picked only where they show; turning wall layers off shows them.

### 5.2 Wall framing

- **One box per piece,** by the served orientation rule (artifacts.md §6, "VS-02b"; ART-003 §6.2): `section_through_wall_m` along the wall's `outward_xy`, `section_in_plane_m` in the wall's plane, perpendicular to the axis. The wall's `outward_xy` is read from its `walls` entry by `wall_id`. A pure function next to `memberMatrix` implements it, with its own test against the rule.
- **Colors:** the three framing groups (§4.2). Tooltip: the kind, its section, and its key, from the assembly schedule joined on `piece_id`, or the kind alone without it.
- **Counts on Reference House 001 v3:** 20 walls (60 layer solids, 20 voids) and 79 pieces with straw bale; 80 layer solids and 246 pieces with stud infill.

### 5.3 Picking and drawing order

- Connection glyphs stay drawn after everything, without depth testing, and are picked first (D062).
- Otherwise a ray picks the **nearest visible** object among members, wall layers, opening pick surfaces, and framing boxes. A wall layer picks its wall. Objects hidden by a switch are neither drawn nor picked.

## 6. The two switches and legends (item 7)

- **"Wall layers"** hides the plan's wall bands and opening marks and the 3D layer solids and opening outlines. **"Wall framing"** hides the plan's framing marks and the 3D framing boxes. Both are on by default, in both views, and remembered per browser (`homedesign.wallLayers` and `homedesign.wallFraming` in `localStorage`, through `app/preferences.ts`, falling back to "on" when storage fails), as D066 decided and as the Connections switch works (D062).
- **Display only:** neither switch changes a schedule, the estimate, or a command. Turning a switch off clears a selection of what it hides.
- **Placement:** beside the Connections switch, above both views, with one hint: "Wall layers are drawn opaque. Turn them off to see the framing; turn both off to see the timber frame."
- **Legends:** under each view, beside the roles and connection types: the layer functions present (in 3D) or "Walls" (in the plan), and the framing groups present, each with its swatch. A legend reads the payload, not a fixed list, and omits what a switch hides.

## 7. Selection (item 8)

The selection (UI-003 §6) gains three kinds:

- **What can be selected:** a wall (its plan band, a 3D layer, or its row in the walls table); an opening (its plan mark, its 3D outline, or its row in the openings table); one or more wall framing pieces (a plan mark, a 3D box, or a row of the piece list). Members and connections stay as they are.
- **Highlights:**
  - a selected wall: its band and its layer solids, and its row;
  - a selected opening: its mark, outline, and row; as a secondary highlight, its framing pieces (assembly schedule rows whose `opening_id` is the opening) in both views and the piece list;
  - selected pieces: their marks, boxes, and rows.
- **Joins come from the same revision's assembly schedule.** When it is not `CURRENT` for the views' revision, only the picked objects themselves are highlighted.
- **The Walls tab follows the selection** (item 1): selecting an opening makes it the tab's current opening, and selecting a wall makes it the wall for a new opening.
- **Renderer boundary unchanged** (UI-001 §6): the workspace computes the highlighted IDs and passes them in; the views read only their payloads and report picks.
- **Clearing:** as today (picking the same thing again, empty plan space, a plane change, a commit), and when a switch hides the selection.

## 8. The Walls tab (items 1, 9–11)

The tab list becomes **Footprint · Stories · Roof · Grid · Frame · Sections · Connections · Walls** (item 1). Everything in UI-002 §4 holds: tabs stay mounted, typed values are kept, **Reset** restores stored values, one command is in flight at a time, and editors appear on the head only. The tab has two parts: the wall assembly, then the openings.

### 8.1 Wall assembly (item 9)

- **One select** of the project's assembly definitions, set to `WallSystem.default_assembly_definition_id`, each shown by its family label ("Straw bale", "Stud infill", §12).
- **Under it, the chosen definition's build-up,** read from the head snapshot's `AssemblyDefinition` record (model data; both definitions are in the snapshot, D067): its layers exterior to interior with material and thickness, the total thickness, and its framing (stud section and spacing, girts, header section, jack threshold width; or buck section). The assembly schedule lists only the definition in use (ART-003 §12 item 6); this panel lets the user see the alternative before previewing it. No thermal figure is shown here: figures are derived, and only the schedule has them.
- **Preview,** enabled when the select differs from the stored value, sends `SetWallAssembly {assembly_definition_id}` through the preview and confirmation (consequential, D068).
- **Note:** "Every wall uses this assembly. Switching regenerates all wall framing; timber members and connections do not change."
- **Pricing note,** as in the Sections and Connections tabs: "Wall layers are priced per square foot or meter (`assembly.<label>.<layer>`) and wall framing per foot or meter (`wallframing.<kind>.<section>`). After switching, the new assembly's lines are unpriced until you price them, and the old assembly's rows become unused." With a **Set unit prices** link.
- **Errors:** a detail at `args.assembly_definition_id`, or naming it in `related_paths` (for example `GIRT_CROSSES_BRACE` at the stud infill definition's `girt_count`, or an opening that no longer fits), is shown under the select, with the opening named (§8.3).

### 8.2 Openings (item 10)

**The list:** every opening of the head snapshot, grouped by wall in the walls' key order, each row showing its label (§12) and rough-opening size. Choosing a row makes it the current opening; so does selecting an opening anywhere (§7).

**The form** for the current opening, or for a new one:

| Field | Input | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wall | A select of the 20 walls by label | For a new opening; defaults to the selected wall. Read-only for an existing opening (its wall does not change, MODEL-006 §8.2) |
| Kind | Window or Door | For a new opening. Read-only for an existing one |
| Width, Height | Lengths (UI-001 §7) | "Rough opening" |
| Sill height | A length | For a window, above the wall's lower datum. For a door, fixed at 0 and disabled (the server requires 0, D069) |
| Offset | A length | "From the {west or south} post axis to the rough opening's start", from the wall's axis (D080: walls along X start at their west post, walls along Y at their south post) |

- **Center in wall:** a button that sets the offset to `(wall length − width) / 2`, from the head snapshot's wall (`start_m`, `end_m`) and the width field. It is an input helper like the grid's "midpoint" (UI-002 §6.3): the value is sent as computed and the server checks it.
- **Stored values:** untouched lengths send their stored doubles exactly (`useStoredFields`). An untouched **Apply** is a no-op, which the notice reports (D045).
- **Commands, applied directly** (routine, D069; UI-002 §5, approved there as item 2): **Apply** sends `SetOpening {opening_id, width_m, height_m, sill_height_m, offset_m}` for an existing opening, or **Add opening** sends `AddOpening {wall_id, opening_kind, width_m, height_m, sill_height_m, offset_m}`. **Remove** sends `RemoveOpening {opening_id}`. Each commits without a preview and shows the workspace's "Saved as revision N" notice with **Undo**, which goes through the preview, as today. After an add, the new opening becomes current.
- **Hints:** "Sizes are rough openings. The framing around it (bucks; or king studs, jack studs above {threshold}, and a header) must fit inside the wall, clear of the knee braces, and apart from the wall's other openings. The server checks this." The threshold is the stud infill definition's, from the snapshot.
- **Errors:** details are placed on fields by their argument paths (`args.width_m`, `args.offset_m`, and so on), which the server adds to every fit detail (MODEL-006 §5.2): `OPENING_OUTSIDE_WALL`, `OPENING_CROSSES_BRACE`, `OPENINGS_OVERLAP`, `UNSUPPORTED_VALUE` (a door's sill). The other opening in an overlap is named (§8.3).

Measured on Reference House 001 v3 (straw bale, through the service while drafting): moving the 5'-0" window in the south-west Level 0 wall to offset 0 is rejected with `OPENING_CROSSES_BRACE` and `OPENING_OUTSIDE_WALL` at its `offset_m`, with `args.offset_m` related; adding a 2'-0" window at offset 2.4 m in that wall is rejected with `OPENINGS_OVERLAP`. Narrowing that window to 4'-0" (the threshold) with stud infill updates its wall and its framing: 1 piece added, 4 removed (its jacks among them), 6 updated.

### 8.3 Commands, paths, and messages (item 11)

- **Tabs of commands:** `SetWallAssembly`, `AddOpening`, `SetOpening`, `RemoveOpening` → Walls (`TAB_OF_COMMAND`), so a commit rebuilds the tab.
- **Cross-tab pointers** (UI-002 §7): `Opening`, `WallSystem`, `AssemblyDefinition`, `Wall`, and `WallFramingPiece` paths map to the Walls tab (`TAB_OF_KIND`). So a footprint, story, grid, or frame edit that an opening no longer fits points to the Walls tab. An opening error names the frame values that bound its wall, so it also points to those tabs (Footprint, Grid, Stories, Frame), as the brace fit already does; nothing changes in that rule.
- **Paths read as names:** in the dialog's detail list and in field errors, a path into an opening, a wall, or a definition is shown with its label (§12) before the field, for example "Window 3' 0" × 4' 0", south wall, Level 0, x0–x1: offset", with the raw path in its tooltip. Labels come from the head snapshot.
- **Detail text** (`DETAIL_TEXT`): `OPENING_OUTSIDE_WALL` "The opening's framing does not fit inside the wall", `OPENING_CROSSES_BRACE` "The opening's framing meets a knee brace", `OPENINGS_OVERLAP` "The opening's framing overlaps another opening's", `GIRT_CROSSES_BRACE` "A girt would cross a knee brace". Codes stay visible, as today.

### 9.2 Openings removed with their walls (item 2)

A grid edit that removes a wall removes its openings (D069, MODEL-006 §8.5). That includes **re-identifying** a grid line, which changes no geometry: wall keys name their grid lines (D080), so the walls on the line are replaced and their openings are removed (ART-003 §8.3, §17). Measured on Reference House 001 v3: removing grid line `y1` and adding a new one at the same position (the Grid tab's Remove, then its midpoint button) previews 115 members and 212 connections replaced, the 8 east and west walls added and removed under new keys, 32 buck pieces removed, and **8 windows removed**, each a `changes` entry with `after: null`.

**Approved (item 2, [D088](../../product/decisions/D088-vs02b-removed-openings-warning.md)):** the dialog lists them in a warning block above **Confirm**: "These 8 openings will be removed with their walls. Undo restores them." followed by each opening's label and size (from the head snapshot). Confirm stays as it is: the edit is already consequential, and the list is D069's "the preview lists them".

## 10. The assembly schedule (items 3, 13)

A new panel, **Assembly schedule**, below the timber schedule, from the same revision's `assembly_schedule` envelope (artifacts.md §7A). When it is not `CURRENT`, it says so, as the timber schedule does. In order:

1. **Warning and source:** the envelope's `CLIMATE_DETAILS_UNVERIFIED` message, read from the envelope and never hard-coded (as `PreliminaryBanner` reads `PRELIMINARY_UNVERIFIED`), then the definition's `unverified_climate_details` as a list under it. The revision and the stored units.
2. **The definition in use:** family label and label, total thickness, installation note, and a layer table, exterior to interior: layer (material name), function, thickness, thermal conductivity or resistance, and vapor permeance. Each declared value shows its value and unit with its source, date, and note; a `null` reads "Not declared". The framing layer's row shows its framing material and its conductivity the same way, and its parameters (sections by label, spacing, girt count, jack threshold width).
3. **Thermal figures** (`thermal`): the method's `id` and its `notes`, verbatim and in order; for the definition, the center-of-cavity, framing-path, and parallel-path figures, and for all walls the area-weighted figure, each as a U-value and an R-value with units (item 3). An unknown figure reads "Not computed" and is never 0; its `missing_inputs` are listed once, as "Missing declared values: {material} ({what would supply it})", for example "Lime plaster (conductivity or resistance)" and "Spruce-pine-fir framing (conductivity)". On Reference House 001 v3, every figure but the framing fraction is "Not computed".
4. **Walls** (20 rows): wall label, length, height, gross, opening, and net area, framing fraction (as a percentage to two decimals, for example "2.31%"), and U, R, and UA, each "—" when `null`. A totals row from `totals`.
5. **Openings** (20 rows): label, kind, rough opening, area, and "with jacks" from `has_jacks`.
6. **Framing takeoff** (`framing_aggregates`): kind label, section, count, total length.
7. **Layer quantities** (`layer_aggregates`): definition, layer (material), function, thickness, area, volume. The framing layer's row is its fill net of framing (D082), and says so in its tooltip.
8. **Wall framing pieces** (`wall_framing`, 79 or 246 rows), scrollable like the member table: key, kind, section, species, length, and its opening's label.
9. **Assumptions:** the nine lines, verbatim and in order.

Areas use `formatArea`; volumes a new `formatVolume` (ft³ or m³); lengths `formatLength`. Nothing is summed or derived in the browser.

**Thermal units (item 3, approved as [D089](../../product/decisions/D089-vs02b-thermal-display-units.md)).** The payload's figures are SI. The figures follow the existing units switch. Metric shows them as served: U in W/(m²·K), R in m²·K/W. Imperial shows U in Btu/(h·ft²·°F) and R in h·ft²·°F/Btu, converted for display with 1 W/(m²·K) = 0.17611 Btu/(h·ft²·°F) (the IT Btu; equivalently 1 m²·K/W = 5.678263 h·ft²·°F/Btu), and declared conductivities in Btu·in/(h·ft²·°F) (1 W/(m·K) = 6.933472 Btu·in/(h·ft²·°F)). Declared vapor permeances stay in SI, as served, in both: moisture values are recorded only (D071), and converting them would add a unit choice (US perms) that nothing here needs. Every figure carries its unit; none is shown as a bare "R-30". The conversion is display only, like lengths (UI-001 §7).
