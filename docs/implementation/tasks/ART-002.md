## 1. Objective and user-visible behavior

Present the complete version 2 frame and its connections in all four artifacts, as [D054](../../product/decisions/D054-vs02-connection-catalog.md) ("Where connections appear") and [D055](../../product/decisions/D055-pre-release-schema-replacement.md) ("additive version 2 payloads") require:

- **`plan2d`:** every member role, not just posts and beams, and a marker at each connection;
- **`mesh3d`:** a box orientation rule that covers every role, and a marker at each connection;
- **`timber_schedule`:** a connection list, counts per junction class and type, catalog descriptions, and assumption lines that replace `NO_CONNECTIONS`, including the [D059](../../product/decisions/D059-vs02-junction-rules-connection-type-command.md) line: **joints without a junction class (post splices, sill joints) are not counted**;
- **`preliminary_estimate`:** one line per connection type, counted "each" and priced by a `connection.<TYPE>` row;
- **price books:** rows in meters or each.

**User-visible after this task:**
- The API's plan carries sills, joists, braces, rafters, and purlins, and every payload but the estimate carries the 312 connections of Reference House 001 v2.
- The estimate lists three connection lines for the reference house, and a price book can price them per piece.
- In the browser (§12 item 10): the plan draws the new roles in one neutral style, the estimate table shows "each" lines, and the price-book form accepts per-piece prices. Connection markers, a connection table, role styling, and the type editor wait for `UI-003`.

Nothing here claims capacity, adequacy, sizes, or fasteners (AC2-07). Lengths stay reference-axis lengths.

## 4. `plan2d`

### 4.1 Planes and members

Each plane keeps `posts` and `beams` as they are, and gains one array per new role, with the beam shape `{member_id, generation_key, start_xy_m, end_xy_m}` (§4.3 drops `section_id`). Every plane has every array; arrays with no members are empty, as `posts` on the eave plane is today.

| Plane | `level_id` | `z_m` | Members, by key | Reference House 001 v2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `level-0` | Level 0 | `L0.elevation_m` | `posts` (`/level-0/post`), `sills` (`/plane-L0/sill`) | 12 posts, 17 sills |
| `level-1` | Level 1 | `L1.elevation_m` | `posts` (`/level-1/post`), `beams` (`/plane-L1/beam`), `joists` (`/plane-L1/joist`), `braces` (`/plane-L1/brace/`) | 12, 17, 30 joists, 34 braces |
| `eave` | `null` | `z_eave` | `beams` (`/plane-eave/beam`), `braces` (`/plane-eave/brace/`) | 17 beams, 34 braces |
| `roof` (new, §12 item 8, approved) | `null` | `z_eave` | `rafters` (`frame/roof/{x}/{slope}/principal-rafter`), `purlins` (`frame/roof/{xa}-{xb}/{slope}/p{i}/purlin`) | 8 rafters, 12 purlins |

- **Total:** 193 member entries, one per member, so the plan inventory again equals the snapshot's (AC2-02). The integration invariant goes back to comparing every member.
- **Assignment:** the role picks the array, and the key segment picks the plane. A member whose role and key match no row raises, so the derivation is stored `FAILED` (ART-001), as GEOM-001 does today. Matching on role first matters: a joist key also contains `/plane-L1/`, which is why the current code filters roles.
- **Positions:** every linear member's `start_xy_m` and `end_xy_m` are the plan projections of its `start_m` and `end_m`, as for beams. A brace projects to a segment of length `L` along its beam from the post. A rafter projects from the wall line to the ridge line (D058: no tails).
- **Ordering:** each array sorted by `generation_key` in code-unit order, as today.
- **Plane order:** `level-0`, `level-1`, `eave`, `roof`.
- **The roof plane's `z_m`** is `z_eave`: the plate line, where the roof framing bears (D058). Rafters and purlins rise from it, and their plan positions are projections.

The interim note in artifacts.md §5 (MODEL-005 §9) is removed.

### 4.2 Connection markers

Each plane gains `connections`, one marker per `Connection`:

```json
{"connection_id": "…", "generation_key": "connection/post-beam/frame/x1/y0/level-0/post/frame/axis-x/y0/x0-x1/plane-L1/beam",
 "junction_class": "post-beam", "connection_type": "PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON", "xy_m": [3.6576, 0.0]}
```

- **Plane:** the plane of the **entering** member (`member_ids[1]`, D059), by §4.1. So `post-sill` is on `level-0`; `post-beam`, `brace`, and `joist-beam` on their member's plane; `rafter-foot`, `rafter-peak`, and `purlin-rafter` on `roof`.
- **Position:** `xy_m` is the plan projection of `location_m`.
- **Ordering:** by `generation_key`, code-unit order.
- **Reference House 001 v2:** 12 on `level-0` (post-sill), 162 on `level-1` (34 post-beam, 68 brace, 60 joist-beam), 102 on `eave` (34 post-beam, 68 brace), and 36 on `roof` (4 peak, 8 foot, 24 purlin-rafter). Total 312.
- **Coincident markers:** up to eight markers share one plan point (an interior Level 1 station: four beam ends and four brace feet). The payload keeps all of them. How to draw them is `UI-003`'s concern.
- **No `member_ids`** in the plan: the schedule lists them (§6.1), and clients join on `connection_id`.

### 4.3 No `section_id` in the plan (§12 item 2)

Posts and beams currently carry `section_id`. [D056](../../product/decisions/D056-dependency-selectors.md) says `plan2d` reads member axes and roles, "not sections", and that changing a role's section leaves `plan2d` unaffected. `SetMemberSection` changes every member's `section_id` for the role, so a plan that carries `section_id` changes payload on a section change. Under AC2-04 ("if and only if its payload changes") that would force `plan2d` into `impacted_artifacts`, contradicting D056. The plan therefore drops `section_id` from posts and beams, and the new arrays never carry it.

- **Who is affected:** `Plan2dView.tsx` does not read `section_id`, and clients that need a section join on `member_id` with the schedule or mesh.
- **Alternative:** keep `section_id`, and amend D056 so that section changes impact `plan2d`. That keeps the field, but makes every section edit re-derive the plan for no visible change.

### 4.4 Unchanged

`footprint`, `roof_plan`, `dimensions` (exactly two), and `units`.

## 5. `mesh3d`

### 5.1 Box orientation for every role

artifacts.md §6 currently specifies beams and posts only. It is restated as one rule that the client (`meshScene.ts`) already implements:

- **Vertical axis (posts):** `section_width_m` along +X, `section_depth_m` along +Y.
- **Any other axis:** `section_width_m` is horizontal and perpendicular to the axis's plan projection. `section_depth_m` is along `axis × width`, perpendicular to both, taken with a non-negative Z component.

What this gives each role:

| Role | Width | Depth | Matches |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BEAM`, `SILL`, `FLOOR_JOIST` | Horizontal, across the member | Vertical | D053: joists "depth vertical" |
| `PRINCIPAL_RAFTER` | Along X (across the bent) | Perpendicular to the roof plane | D053: "depth perpendicular to the roof plane" |
| `KNEE_BRACE` | Perpendicular to its beam's vertical plane | In that plane, perpendicular to the brace | D058: braces lie in their beam's plane |
| `PURLIN` | Horizontal, along Y | Vertical (plumb) | §12 item 9, approved |

No payload field changes: the boxes keep `section_width_m` and `section_depth_m` only.

### 5.2 Connection markers

A top-level `connections` array beside `members`:

```json
{"connection_id": "…", "generation_key": "connection/rafter-peak/…", "junction_class": "rafter-peak",
 "connection_type": "BRIDLE", "location_m": [0.0, 3.6576, 8.5344]}
```

- **Fields:** `location_m` is the record's, copied exactly. There is no size, primitive, or shape: the payload says where a connection is and what type it has, and the client chooses a marker (D054: "a small marker", colored by type).
- **Ordering:** by `generation_key`. Count and IDs equal the snapshot's connections (312 for the reference house).

### 5.3 Unchanged

`members` (one box per member, in key order), `envelope`, and `units`.

## 6. `timber_schedule`

### 6.1 Connection list

A `connections` array, one row per `Connection`, sorted by `generation_key`:

```json
{"connection_id": "…", "generation_key": "connection/joist-beam/…", "junction_class": "joist-beam",
 "connection_type": "HOUSED_DOVETAIL", "member_ids": ["<receiving>", "<entering>"], "quantity": 1}
```

- **`member_ids`** is the record's `[receiving, entering]` pair (D054, D059), so the schedule says which members each joint joins.
- **No `location_m`:** positions belong to the views. D056's schedule selector reads connection "types and classes", and keeping positions out means a change that only moves the frame (an origin or overhang edit) leaves the connection rows unchanged.
- **`quantity`** is 1, as for member rows.

### 6.2 Counts per junction class and type

A `connection_aggregates` array: one row per (`junction_class`, `connection_type`) pair that has at least one connection, with `count`, sorted by class, then type, in code-unit order.

- **Why pairs:** D054 asks for "counts per type and junction class". A class has one type at a time (D054), so the pairs give both: the count per class is its row, and the count per type is the sum of its rows, which the estimate uses (§7.1).
- **Zero rows are omitted**, as member aggregates omit absent roles. With both brace planes off, there is no `brace` row.
- **Reconciliation:** the counts sum to the number of connection rows and of `Connection` records, and each class's count equals CONN-001 §6's formula.

### 6.4 Assumptions

`NO_CONNECTIONS` ("Joinery and machining are not included.") is no longer true and is replaced. The list becomes, in this order:

| Code | Message |
|---|---|
| `REFERENCE_AXIS_LENGTH` (unchanged) | Lengths are reference-axis distances, not fabrication cut lengths. |
| `CONNECTIONS_REPRESENTATIONAL` | Connections are listed by type and count only. Types are representational: no capacity, sizes, fasteners, or machining. |
| `UNCLASSED_JOINTS_NOT_COUNTED` | Joints without a junction class, such as post splices and sill joints at grid stations, are not counted. |

The third line is the one D059 requires. Its wording follows D059's example, and adds "joints without a junction class" so the rule reads as general, not as a list.

## 7. `preliminary_estimate` and price books

### 7.1 Line items

Line items gain a `kind` discriminator:

```json
{"kind": "timber", "key": "timber.POST.8x8", "role": "POST", "nominal_section": "8x8",
 "quantity_value": 69.4944, "quantity_unit": "m", "unit_price_minor": null, "amount_minor": null, "priced": false}
{"kind": "connection", "key": "connection.PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON", "connection_type": "PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON",
 "quantity_value": 248, "quantity_unit": "each", "unit_price_minor": 4500, "amount_minor": 1116000, "priced": true}
```

- **Timber lines:** unchanged, plus `kind: "timber"`.
- **Connection lines:** one per connection type with a non-zero count (D054: "one line per type"). `quantity_value` is the integer sum of that type's `connection_aggregates` counts, and `quantity_unit` is `each`. There is no `role` or `nominal_section`.
- **Amounts:** `unit_price_minor × count`, an exact integer product, so no rounding applies. The function shared with timber lines gives the same result.
- **Ordering:** by `key` in code-unit order, as today, so connection lines precede timber lines.
- **Coverage and totals:** the artifacts.md §8.1 rule is unchanged and applies to every emitted line. A book that prices only timber is therefore `partial` once connection lines exist, with `grand_total_minor: null` and `COST_INPUTS_MISSING`. Unpriced lines are never 0 (AC2-06).
- **Same revision:** quantities still come from the schedule for the same `source_revision_id` (artifacts.md §8.1).

Reference House 001 v2 with the default types has ten lines: `connection.BRIDLE` (4), `connection.HOUSED_DOVETAIL` (60), `connection.PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON` (248), then the seven timber lines of §6.5.

**Alternative:** one flat line shape, with `role` and `nominal_section` nullable and a nullable `connection_type`. It is simpler to render, but the types no longer say which fields belong together.

### 7.2 Assumptions on the estimate

The estimate payload gains `assumptions`, a copy of its schedule's list (§6.4). Its quantities come from that schedule, so the reader of a per-piece total sees that unclassed joints are missing, as D059 requires for "the schedule and estimate".

**Alternative:** an envelope warning on the estimate. Warnings, though, report conditions such as missing prices, and this is a standing basis of the numbers.

### 7.3 Price-book rows in meters or each

```json
{"key": "timber.POST.8x8", "unit": "m", "unit_price_minor": 3150}
{"key": "connection.PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON", "unit": "each", "unit_price_minor": 4500}
```

- **`unit`** is `m` or `each`.
- **Unit must match the key family:** a `timber.` key needs `m` and a `connection.` key needs `each`. A mismatch is rejected with a new stage 2 code, `PRICE_UNIT_MISMATCH`, at `rows[k].unit`, with `related_paths` `["rows[k].key"]` and the unit as `value`. It shares stage 2 with `DUPLICATE_PRICE_KEY`, so both are reported together, in row order.
  - **Why reject:** a connection priced per meter, or a member per piece, would silently misprice a line if it were applied, and would silently drop a price if it were ignored.
  - **Other keys** keep today's behavior: accepted with either unit, ignored, and named in `PRICE_ROWS_UNUSED`.
- **Unused rows:** a `connection.` row for a type with no connections (for example `STEEL_KNIFE_PLATE` before any `SetConnectionType`) is named in `PRICE_ROWS_UNUSED`, as today. Switching a class's type can therefore turn a priced line into an unused row and a new unpriced line. That is the honest result, and coverage shows it.
- **Unchanged:** book-level metadata, USD only, versions, the fingerprint inputs (artifacts.md §2.3), and setting a book outside the model command path.
- **Large amounts:** an amount outside ±(2⁵³ − 1) fails the estimate's validation, as it already does for timber lines. The implementer confirms this is reported as `DERIVATION_FAILED`, not as a server error.

## 9. Browser: the smallest changes that keep it correct (§12 item 10, approved)

The new payloads break three things in the current client:

- the estimate table labels every line `role nominal_section` and formats every quantity as a length;
- the price-book form builds a row for every estimate line, converts its price from per foot to per meter, and submits `unit: "m"`, which §7.3 now rejects for `connection.` keys;
- the plan view draws only `posts` and `beams`.

Interim changes, all in existing components:

- **Estimate table** (`panels.tsx`): connection lines show the type's description, a count, and a price "each". Timber lines are unchanged.
- **Price-book form** (`PriceBookForm.tsx`): `each` rows take a price per piece, with no unit conversion, and are submitted with `unit: "each"`. Unused stored rows keep their unit.
- **Plan view** (`Plan2dView.tsx`): the new arrays are drawn as lines in one neutral style, as MODEL-005 §9 did for 3D. The `roof` plane gets a tab like the others.
- **Not here:** markers in 2D and 3D, a connection table in the schedule view, role colors and symbols, the Frame tab, the type editor, and connection counts in the confirmation dialog stay in `UI-003`. The schedule view already lists the payload's assumptions, so the new lines appear without a change.

**Alternative:** only stop the breakage. The form leaves out connection lines while keeping their stored rows, and the table labels them. The price-book form's "each" rows (AC2-08) then stay with `UI-003`. That is less work here, but the API could then set prices that the browser cannot edit.

|---|---|
| `plan2d` | `Footprint` (origin, width, depth, rotation); `Level` elevations and Level 1's story height (`z_eave`); `GableRoof.eave_overhang_m`; each member's ID, key, role, and the x and y of `start_m` and `end_m`; each connection's ID, key, class, type, the x and y of `location_m`, and the entering member's key. **Not** sections, materials, member or connection `z`, or pitch |
| `mesh3d` | Footprint, levels, and the whole `GableRoof` (pitch included); each member's ID, key, role, axis, and its section's `actual_width_m` and `actual_depth_m`; each connection's ID, key, class, type, and `location_m` |
| `timber_schedule` | Each member's ID, key, role, axis (for its length), section (`nominal_label`), and material (`species`, `grade`); each connection's ID, key, class, type, and `member_ids`; the catalog constant. **Not** connection locations |
| `preliminary_estimate` | The schedule's `aggregates`, `connection_aggregates`, and `assumptions`; the price book |

**Two consequences for `DEP-001`** under AC2-04's "if and only if":

- **Pitch:** no plan position depends on pitch. Rafter and purlin plan positions depend only on the footprint and the purlin fractions. So a pitch-only `SetGableRoofParameters` leaves `plan2d` unchanged and must not list it. That is narrower than D056's example ("roof … changes still affect all four"), and the criterion governs.
- **Projections:** a selector that hashes member axes in 3D would mark `plan2d` impacted by changes that move only `z`. The plan's selector should hash the plan projections and plane heights it uses.
