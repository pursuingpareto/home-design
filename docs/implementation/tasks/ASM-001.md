## 1. Objective and user-visible behavior

Implement `vs02b-walls-1`, the only wall rule, and register it behind MODEL-006's wall-rule interface (MODEL-006 §7). It is a pure, deterministic function from a version 3 snapshot to:

- **walls,** one in every perimeter bay of each story ([D065](../../product/decisions/D065-vs02b-wall-placement.md));
- **wall framing,** one record per piece ([D066](../../product/decisions/D066-vs02b-wall-framing.md)):
  - layout studs and girts;
  - kings, jacks above the threshold, headers, rough sills, and cripples in stud infill walls;
  - bucks in straw bale walls;
- **each opening's framed rectangle and each wall's girt heights,** which MODEL-006's fit rules read ([D076](../../product/decisions/D076-vs02b-framed-opening-geometry.md)).

The task also delivers:

- the template's two assembly definitions, with their sections and materials ([D072](../../product/decisions/D072-reference-house-001-v3-design-intent.md));
- the Reference House 001 v3 fixture and packaged template, built by script with a drift check;
- an ECMAScript oracle for walls and wall framing.

**User-visible after the merge:** a new project is Reference House 001 v3, with 20 straw bale walls, 1 door, 19 windows, and 79 buck pieces. `SetWallAssembly` to stud infill gives **246** pieces (§8.2). Frame edits keep walls and their openings wherever the bay survives. No artifact shows walls yet (`ART-003`), and the browser has no wall controls yet (`UI-004`).

## 4. Walls

### 4.1 Notation and numeric evaluation

The frame's rules hold ([generation-rules.md](../../reference-projects/vermont-timber-house/generation-rules.md), "Numeric evaluation"): IEEE-754 doubles, evaluated **exactly as written, with ordinary precedence, left to right**, no fused multiply-add, and no rounding or snapping. The wall rule reads no roof value, so it has **no `tan` exception**: every coordinate is bit-identical across implementations.

```text
x(line), y(line)        as the frame rule: origin + dimension * fraction
For a wall on level i:  z_lo = level[i].elevation_m        H = level[i].story_height_m
For a wall in bay a-b:  A = x(a) or y(a)                   B = x(b) or y(b)          W = B - A
L = knee_brace_leg_m    braced = knee_braces_level_1 (level 0) or knee_braces_eave (level 1)
ε = 1e-6 m
```

- **Wall-local coordinates** (MODEL-006 §5): `u` along the wall from the start post axis, and `v` up from the lower datum. A point `(u, v)` maps to `(A + u, y, z_lo + v)` on a wall along X, and to `(x, A + u, z_lo + v)` on a wall along Y.
- **Ends on post axes use `A` and `B` directly,** not `A + W`, so a wall and its girts end exactly on the frame's post axes.
- **The upper datum is `z_lo + H`.** On Level 1 that is the frame's `z_eave` expression exactly. On Level 0 it equals `z1` within the 1e-6 m of D033's consistency check, and exactly on the reference house (0 + 3.048).

### 4.2 Placement

One wall per perimeter bay and story ([D065](../../product/decisions/D065-vs02b-wall-placement.md)): every adjacent pair of X lines on the first and last Y lines, and every adjacent pair of Y lines on the first and last X lines, on both levels. The count is `2 · ((nx − 1) + (ny − 1))` per story; 20 on the reference house.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `start_m`, `end_m` | `(A, y, z_lo)` → `(B, y, z_lo)` along X; `(x, A, z_lo)` → `(x, B, z_lo)` along Y. The start is the lower station: west, or south (D066) |
| `height_m` | `H` |
| `level_id` | the level's ID |
| `assembly_definition_id` | `WallSystem.default_assembly_definition_id` (D068) |
| `framing_ids` | its pieces, ascending by key (MODEL-006 §4.4) |

**Brace planes:** each wall lies under its perimeter beam, so with its plane on it has one brace at each top corner, from `(0, H − L)` to `(L, H)` and from `(W, H − L)` to `(W − L, H)` (MODEL-006 §5.2). On the reference house these are 40 of the 68 braces (D065).

## 5. Wall framing

### 5.1 Inputs from the definition

Each wall's pieces come from its definition's framing layer (MODEL-006 §4.3; D066, D076). Every piece's `material_id` is the layer's `framing_material_id`.

| Symbol | Stud infill (`STUD`) | Straw bale (`BUCK`) |
|---|---|---|
| `S` | `stud_spacing_m` | — |
| `n` | `girt_count` | 0 (no girts) |
| `t` | stud section `actual_width_m`: studs, kings, jacks, cripples, rough sills | — |
| `t_g` | girt section `actual_width_m` | — |
| `d` | header section `actual_depth_m` (vertical) | — |
| `T` | `jack_threshold_width_m` | — |
| `b` | — | buck section `actual_width_m`: jambs, head, sill |

An opening has rough opening `[o, o + w] × [s, s + h]`, from `offset_m`, `width_m`, `sill_height_m`, and `height_m`. A door has `s = 0` and no sill piece.

**Where a vertical piece ends at the top** (D066: a stud that meets a brace axis ends on it):

```text
top(u) = (H - L) + u          if braced and u < L - ε         (start brace)
       = (H - L) + (W - u)    if braced and u > W - L + ε     (end brace)
       = H                    otherwise
```

The brace fit guarantees `W ≥ 2L` (MODEL-005), so at most one case applies.

### 5.2 Layout studs and girts (stud infill)

- **Layout positions** `p_k = k * S`, for `k = 1, 2, …` while `A + k * S < B − ε`, exactly as joists are placed (D066). No stud lies on a post axis.
- **A layout stud** is `(p_k, 0) → (p_k, top(p_k))`, unless an opening claims its position (§5.4).
- **Girts** at `v_i = H * f_i`, with `f_i = i / (n + 1)`, for `i = 1 … n`, written as the purlins are. A girt runs from the start post axis to the end post axis, cut where it crosses an opening (§5.5).
- **Girts are let in** across every vertical piece they cross: layout studs, and the kings, jacks, and cripples of openings they pass above or below. Neither piece is cut, and the notches are not modeled (D066).

### 5.3 Opening framing

With `j = 1` when `w > T + ε` (jacks) and `j = 0` otherwise. The framed rectangle `[u0, u1] × [v0, v1]` is D076's: `u0 = o − (1 + j)·t`, `u1 = o + w + (1 + j)·t`, `v1 = s + h + d`, and `v0 = s − t` for a window or `0` for a door.

**Stud infill** (formulas as written; `t / 2` and `3 * t / 2` as shown):

| Piece | Axis `u` | `v`, start → end | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| `KING`, start side | `o - t / 2` (j = 0) or `o - 3 * t / 2` (j = 1) | `0` → `top(u)` | stud |
| `KING`, end side | `o + w + t / 2` or `o + w + 3 * t / 2` | `0` → `top(u)` | stud |
| `JACK`, each side (j = 1 only) | `o - t / 2`, `o + w + t / 2` | `0` → `s + h` | stud |
| `HEADER` | `o` → `o + w` (j = 0), or `o - t` → `o + w + t` (j = 1) | at `s + h + d / 2` | header |
| `ROUGH_SILL` (window) | `o` → `o + w` | at `s - t / 2` | stud |
| `CRIPPLE`, above | `p_k` | `s + h + d` → `top(p_k)` | stud |
| `CRIPPLE`, below (window) | `p_k` | `0` → `s - t` | stud |

**Straw bale:**

| Piece | Axis `u` | `v`, start → end |
|---|---|---|
| `BUCK_JAMB`, each side | `o - b / 2`, `o + w + b / 2` | `v0` → `v1`, with `v0 = s - b` (window) or `0` (door), `v1 = s + h + b` |
| `BUCK_HEAD` | `o` → `o + w` | at `s + h + b / 2` |
| `BUCK_SILL` (window) | `o` → `o + w` | at `s - b / 2` |

- Kings and jacks sit beside each other, offset from the rough-opening edge by the stud thickness: D066's exception to D058, for wall framing only.
- **Where pieces end** (§13 item 1a, settled): opening pieces meet one another at faces, and every piece meets the frame at a datum or a brace axis. So the header spans the kings' inner faces, the rough sill and the buck head and sill span the rough opening, jacks stop under the header, and buck jambs run the framed rectangle's full height.
- Every piece listed here has `opening_id` set to its opening.

### 5.4 Layout studs at an opening (stud infill)

D066: "a layout stud that falls between the kings is replaced" by cripples, and "a layout stud that would overlap a king is dropped". The rule reads it with each stud's band `[p_k − t/2, p_k + t/2]` (§13 item 1b):

| The band… | Result |
|---|---|
| overlaps no framed range `[u0, u1]` by more than ε | a layout stud |
| overlaps a king's band (`[u0, u0 + t]` or `[u1 − t, u1]`) by more than ε | dropped |
| otherwise, between the kings | an upper cripple; for a window, also a lower cripple, unless the band overlaps a jack by more than ε (the jack carries the rough sill's end there) |

Touching within ε is not overlapping. On the reference house no layout stud overlaps a king or a jack.

### 5.5 Girt cuts (stud infill)

- A girt is **cut** by an opening when its band `[v_i − t_g/2, v_i + t_g/2]` overlaps the framed range `[v0, v1]` by more than ε (§13 item 1c).
- The cut pieces end at the framed rectangle's sides: a piece runs from the start post axis (or the previous cut's `u1`) to the next cut's `u0` (or the end post axis).
- A girt that passes above or below a framed opening is not cut, and is let in across the kings, jacks, and cripples it crosses (§5.2).
- Girt pieces have `opening_id: null` (MODEL-006 §4.5).

### 5.6 What the rule always does

- **No zero-length pieces.** A piece whose axis is no longer than ε is not generated: for example a lower cripple when `s = t`, an upper cripple when the header's top is on the upper datum, or a girt piece when a framed opening touches a post axis.
- **Total on any snapshot that passes stages 1–3.** The rule never raises, even for openings that will fail the fit at stage 4. A piece that would have a negative length is not generated either. Its output for such snapshots is deterministic but otherwise unspecified, because MODEL-006 rejects them before conformance.
- **Order:** walls by key, and each wall's pieces by key (D030).
- **Directions:** vertical pieces run upward, and horizontal pieces run toward the wall's end.
- **Overlaps:** under items 1a–1c, the faces of two pieces in a wall overlap only where a girt crosses a vertical piece. D071's framing fraction counts those crossings once, and nothing else needs deducting (`ART-003`).

### 5.7 `framed_openings` and `girt_heights`

- **`framed_openings`** returns, for each opening, the §5.3 rectangle. It is computed by the same helper the pieces use, so the two cannot disagree.
- **`girt_heights`** returns, for each wall whose definition has girts, the `v_i` above (empty for straw bale).
- **What "agree" means.** MODEL-006 §7 asks for a test that "every piece that frames an opening lies inside that opening's framed rectangle". Under D076, kings run full height and cripples lie above and below the rectangle, so the test is restated as follows (§13 item 7):
  - every piece with that `opening_id` lies within `[u0, u1]` along the wall;
  - the rectangle is the bounding box of the pieces that close it. `u0` and `u1` are the kings' outer faces (with jacks too) or the jambs' outer faces. `v1` is the header's or buck head's top. `v0` is the rough sill's or buck sill's underside, or 0 for a door;
  - every girt piece lies at one of its wall's `girt_heights`.

### 5.8 Worked example

A 3'-0" × 4'-0" window, sill at 3'-0", centered in a 12' Level 0 bay (`H` 10'-0", 3'-0" braces), with stud infill (2×6 at 24", one girt, 2×8 header, 4'-0" threshold). The rough opening is `u` 4'-6" to 7'-6": no jacks. Twelve pieces:

| Piece | `u` | `v` |
|---|---|---|
| Layout studs `s1`, `s5` | 2'-0", 10'-0" | 0 to 9'-0" (they end on the braces) |
| Layout studs `s2`, `s4` | 4'-0", 8'-0" | 0 to 10'-0" |
| Kings | 4'-5¼", 7'-6¾" | 0 to 10'-0" |
| Header | 4'-6" to 7'-6" | at 7'-3⅝" (underside 7'-0", top 7'-7¼") |
| Rough sill | 4'-6" to 7'-6" | at 2'-11¼" (top 3'-0") |
| Cripples at `s3` | 6'-0" | 7'-7¼" to 10'-0" above; 0 to 2'-10½" below |
| Girt pieces | 0 to 4'-4½"; 7'-7½" to 12'-0" | at 5'-0" |

The framed rectangle is `u` 4'-4½" to 7'-7½" and `v` 2'-10½" to 7'-7¼", as D076 states. With straw bale, the same window has four pieces: jambs at 4'-5¼" and 7'-6¾" (from 2'-10½" to 7'-1½"), a head at 7'-0¾", and a sill at 2'-11¼".

## 6. Keys and identity

### 6.1 Key formats (§13 item 3)

| Record | `generation_key` |
|---|---|
| Wall along X | `wall/axis-x/{y}/{xa}-{xb}/level-{i}` |
| Wall along Y | `wall/axis-y/{x}/{ya}-{yb}/level-{i}` |
| Layout stud | `{wall key}/stud/s{k}` |
| Girt piece from the start post | `{wall key}/girt/g{i}/start` |
| Girt piece after an opening | `{wall key}/girt/g{i}/after/{opening id}` |
| Opening framing | `{wall key}/opening/{opening id}/{piece}` |

`{piece}` is one of:

- `king/start`, `king/end`, `jack/start`, `jack/end`, `header`, `rough-sill`;
- `cripple/s{k}/above`, `cripple/s{k}/below`;
- `buck-jamb/start`, `buck-jamb/end`, `buck-head`, `buck-sill`.

- **Tokens:** `{x}`, `{y}`, `{xa}-{xb}`, and `{ya}-{yb}` are grid-line IDs, as in the beam keys (D042). `{i}` is the level index, as in post keys (`level-0`, `level-1`). `{opening id}` is the opening's UUID (routine item 11 of vs-02b.md §6). Ordinals start at 1 and are not padded. `start` and `end` name the side toward the wall's start or end.
- **No collision:** member keys start with `frame/`, connection keys with `connection/`, and wall and framing keys with `wall/`. Every framing key starts with its wall's key followed by `/`, so a framing key never equals a wall key (MODEL-006 §6.2).
- **Length:** at most about 90 characters on the reference house (an opening's cripple key).
- **Examples:** `wall/axis-x/y0/x1-x2/level-0` (the door's wall); `wall/axis-y/x3/y0-y1/level-1/girt/g1/after/<opening id>`.

### 6.2 Identity under each edit

IDs are kept by key and minted from `"generation/" + key` (D041), by MODEL-006's pipeline (§8.5). A wall whose framing changes is also an `updated` wall, because its `framing_ids` change.

| Edit | Walls | Wall framing |
|---|---|---|
| Footprint, or grid fractions with the same line IDs | Every key survives; walls move | Pieces move. Layout studs keep their ordinals; the bay's far end gains or loses studs. Openings keep their offsets, so their framing keys survive |
| Story heights | Every key survives | Girts move (the fractions are of `H`). A girt that starts or stops crossing an opening gains or loses an `after/…` piece |
| Insert or remove a perimeter grid line | The split or merged bays' walls are retired and recreated; their openings are removed (MODEL-006 §8.5) | Retired with their walls |
| `SetFrameParameters`: brace leg or brace plane | No change | Vertical pieces near the posts change length; every key survives |
| Joist spacing, purlins, roof, `SetMemberSection`, `SetConnectionType` | No change | No change. The wall rule reads no roof or member section (D058) |
| `SetWallAssembly` | Every key survives; `assembly_definition_id` changes | Every piece of the old family is retired and the new family's are added |
| `AddOpening` | No change | Its framing is added. Layout studs it claims are retired (§5.4). The girt piece before it survives, shortened, and `after/{id}` is added |
| `SetOpening` | No change | Its framing keys survive and move. Jacks appear or disappear across the threshold. Cripples and retired layout studs follow the kings |
| `RemoveOpening` | No change | The reverse of `AddOpening`. Restored layout studs get **new** IDs (D041); `UndoToRevision` restores the originals (D044) |

**Openings survive by their wall's ID** (D069, MODEL-006 §8.5): every edit in the table except a line insertion or removal on the perimeter keeps every wall key, so openings keep their `wall_id`, offset, and size.

## 13. Proposals for approval

**Your judgment** (one at a time, in this order; approved items become decisions from **D078**, which is free on `main` and on every remote branch, checked 2026-11-21):

1. **Opening framing details** (§5.3–5.5). D066 names the pieces but not where they end, which layout studs become cripples when jacks are present, or when a girt "crosses" an opening. Three parts, settled one at a time and recorded together:
   - **1a. Where pieces end.** **Settled (2026-11-21): faces, as recommended.** *Recommendation:* opening pieces meet one another at **faces**, and every piece meets the frame at a **datum or brace axis**:
     - the header spans the kings' inner faces (`w`, or `w + 2t` with jacks);
     - the rough sill and the buck head and sill span the rough opening (`w`);
     - jacks stop under the header, and cripples stop at the header's top or the rough sill's underside;
     - buck jambs run the framed rectangle's full height;
     - cut girts stop at the kings' outer faces, the framed rectangle's sides.

     So no two opening pieces' faces overlap, and D071's framing fraction needs no deduction beyond girt crossings. *Alternative:* **axis to axis**, as the frame meets: headers and cut girts to the king axes, sills to the jack axes. Each such piece is then `t/2` longer at each end, and the faces overlap at every opening, so `ART-003` must deduct them.
   - **1b. Layout studs at an opening.** *Recommendation:* §5.4's band rule:
     - a stud that overlaps a king (by more than ε) is dropped;
     - a stud between the kings becomes an upper cripple and, for a window, a lower cripple;
     - the lower cripple is dropped where the stud overlaps a jack, which carries the sill's end there.

     *Alternative:* keep both cripples wherever a stud falls between the kings. The lower cripple and the jack then occupy the same space when a stud lands on a jack.

     Neither case occurs on the reference house, so the count (246) is the same under both.
   - **1c. When a girt is cut.** *Recommendation:* when the girt's band (its axis ± half its section) overlaps the framed rectangle's height by more than ε. A girt that passes above or below the rectangle is let in across the kings, jacks, and cripples, as across studs. *Alternative:* when the girt's axis lies within the framed height. That is simpler to state, but a girt just above a header would overlap it by up to half its thickness.
2. **Openings stacked in one wall** (amends MODEL-006 §5.2, refining D069 and D076). MODEL-006's `OPENINGS_OVERLAP` rejects framed rectangles that overlap in both `u` and `v`. So a window above a door, with overlapping `u` ranges, is accepted today. But D066 makes kings continuous, from datum to datum, so the lower opening's kings would pass through the upper opening. *Recommendation:* **framed openings in one wall must not overlap along the wall** (in `u`) by more than ε, in both families. The code and paths are unchanged, and only the test is narrowed. It is simple, and `SetWallAssembly` meets no family-specific rejection. *Alternatives:*
   - the `u`-only rule for stud infill walls only, keeping stacked openings in straw bale walls;
   - kings that stop at another opening's framing, which D066 does not describe.
3. **Key formats** (§6.1):
   - walls `wall/axis-{x|y}/{line}/{a}-{b}/level-{i}`;
   - framing under the wall's key: layout studs `stud/s{k}`, girt pieces named by what they start from (`girt/g{i}/start`, `girt/g{i}/after/{opening id}`), and opening framing `opening/{opening id}/{piece}`.

   *Alternatives:*
   - girt pieces by ordinal (`g1/1`, `g1/2`): adding an opening before a piece renumbers every later piece, so surviving girts trade IDs;
   - opening framing at the top level (`opening/{id}/…`): shorter, but framing no longer sorts under its wall, and routine item 11's "keyed under its wall's key" reads less directly.
4. **Template text** (§7; the question MODEL-006 §13 left to this task). D072 gives no installation notes or climate-detail lists. Both are illustrative, like D072's values, and make no claim (AC-08). *Recommendation:*
   - **`straw-bale`:**
     - `installation_note`: "Straw bales laid flat in courses between the posts, from sill to beam, with a 1½" × 18" buck at every opening. Lime plaster outside and clay plaster inside, applied to the bales. Illustrative build-up for a test house, not a specification."

       - "Bale moisture protection at the sill: a raised base and a capillary break below the first course."
       - "Exterior lime plaster and its rain protection (overhangs, splash zone, or a rain screen) for the site's exposure."
       - "Plaster junctions at posts, beams, and bucks (cracking and air leakage)."
       - "Flashing and sill pans at window and door bucks."
       - "Bale moisture content at installation, and drying through both plasters."
   - **`stud-infill`:**
     - `installation_note`: "2×6 studs at 24" on center between the posts, one mid-height girt let in across them, and kings and headers at every opening (jacks above 4'-0"). Dense-pack cellulose between the studs, sheathing and wood siding outside, gypsum board inside. Illustrative build-up for a test house, not a specification."
