## 1. Objective and user-visible behavior

Show the complete version 2 frame and its connections in the browser, and make the VS-02 commands editable there. This closes AC2-08 and the remaining browser items of [D054](../../product/decisions/D054-vs02-connection-catalog.md) ("Where connections appear"), [D060](../../product/decisions/D060-vs02-artifact-payloads.md) ("stay in `UI-003`"), and [DEP-001](DEP-001.md) §9.2 ("friendly names and connection counts").

**User-visible after this task:**

- **Roles:** each of the seven roles has its own style in the plan and its own color in 3D, with a legend. Knee braces are visible in the plan, not hidden under their beams (§4).
- **Connection markers:** the plan and the 3D view draw a marker at every connection, colored by type, using the positions the payloads serve. Markers at the same point share one glyph (§5, item 10). Markers can be hidden (item 11).
- **Selection:** members and connections can be selected in the plan, the 3D view, or the schedule, and are highlighted in all three. Selecting a connection also highlights its two members (§6).
- **Frame tab:** joist spacing, purlins per slope, knee brace leg, and the two brace switches, through preview and confirmation (`SetFrameParameters`, §7.1).
- **Connection types:** one type per junction class, chosen from the class's two catalog types, through preview and confirmation (`SetConnectionType`, §7.2, items 12 and 13).
- **Schedule:** connection counts per junction class and type, the catalog descriptions of the types in use, and the connection list, with role styling in the member tables (§8).
- **Confirmation dialog:** artifacts by their display names, which ones change and which stay as they are, and connection counts beside the member counts (§9).

Nothing here claims capacity, sizes, or fasteners (AC2-07). Marker glyphs are symbols, not sizes, and every type description shown says "Representational only".

## 5. Connection markers

### 5.1 What is drawn (item 2)

- **Positions as served:** plan markers at `xy_m`, on the plane that carries them (artifacts.md §5); 3D markers at `location_m` (artifacts.md §6). The client computes nothing but the grouping in §5.2.
- **Color by type** (D054): five type colors. Traditional types use warm tones and engineered types cool or grey tones, so the family reads at a glance. The legend lists the types present, by display label, with "traditional" or "engineered".
- **Symbol, not size:** a plan marker is a small circle of fixed size in plan units, like today's post mark (`POST_MARK_M`, "a symbol, not a measured section"). A 3D marker is a small solid of fixed world size. Neither size means anything about the joint (AC2-07), and the code says so where the constants are defined.
- **Tooltip:** the junction class and type display labels, and the generation key, as members show theirs today.
- **Data attributes for tests:** each glyph carries the IDs of its connections and their count; each view reports its total marker count (as `data-member-count` today).

### 5.2 Markers at the same point (item 10, judgment)

Connections meet where axes meet (D058), so many markers share a point. Measured on the UI fixture (Reference House 001 v2):

| View | Markers | Distinct points | Largest group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan, `level-0` | 12 | 12 | 1 |
| Plan, `level-1` | 162 | 91 | 8 (an interior station: four beam ends and four brace feet) |
| Plan, `eave` | 102 | 46 | 8 |
| Plan, `roof` | 36 | 28 | 2 (purlin ends from both sides of a rafter) |
| 3D | 312 | 193 | 4 (beam ends at a post top, with a rafter foot) |

Positions are exactly equal where they coincide (grouping by exact coordinates and by coordinates rounded to 1e-6 m give the same counts).

**Proposal:** one glyph per distinct position.
- A glyph for one connection looks and behaves as in §5.1.
- A glyph for several connections shows their count. It takes the type color when all share one type, and a "mixed" style otherwise. Its tooltip lists each connection.
- Clicking it selects all its connections (§6).
- Grouping is by exact coordinate equality per plan plane, and per 3D point.

**Alternatives:**
- **Offset each marker toward its entering member** by a fixed display distance. Every connection keeps its own glyph, but markers no longer sit at the served position. In the plan a brace foot and its beam's end still coincide, because a brace projects along its beam.
- **Draw every marker, overlapping.** Simplest, but 135 of the 312 plan markers and 119 of the 3D markers are hidden under others, and only the schedule reaches them.

### 5.3 Markers inside members in 3D, and showing or hiding markers (item 11, judgment)

In 3D every connection lies at an axis intersection (D058), inside the member boxes, so a depth-tested marker of any size below the sections is invisible.

**Proposal:**
- **Drawn over the members:** 3D markers are rendered after the members, without depth testing, so they stay visible through the boxes. They are picked before members.
- **One "Connections" switch** for both views, above them, on by default and remembered per browser (as the units preference, `app/preferences.ts`). When it is off, neither view draws markers. The schedule's connection tables are unaffected.

**Alternatives:**
- **Ghost the members** (translucent boxes) whenever markers are shown, with depth-tested markers. Depth reads correctly, but the frame looks washed out whenever connections are on.
- **Markers larger than the sections,** so they protrude. They would read as joint sizes, which D054 rules out.
- **No switch:** markers always on. One less control, but the views cannot be read without 177 plan glyphs and 193 3D glyphs.

## 6. Selection (item 3)

Today the workspace holds one selected member ID, highlighted in the plan, the 3D view, and the schedule (UI-001 §4).

- **What can be selected:** one member, as today, or a set of connections: one from the connection list or a single-connection glyph, or all of a group glyph's connections.
- **Highlights:**
  - a selected member: its plan entry, its 3D box, and its schedule row, as today;
  - selected connections: their glyphs, their rows in the connection list (scrolled to the first), and, as a secondary highlight in a second color, their members in both views and the member table.
- **Members of a connection** come from the same revision's schedule rows (`member_ids`). When the schedule is not `CURRENT` for the views' revision, only the glyphs and rows are highlighted.
- **Renderer boundary unchanged** (UI-001 §6): the workspace computes the highlighted IDs and passes them to `Plan2dView` and `Mesh3dView`, which still read only their payloads and report picks.
- **Clearing:** clicking empty space, or selecting the same thing again, clears the selection, as today. A commit or a plane change clears it.

## 7. Editors

The tab list becomes **Footprint · Stories · Roof · Grid · Frame · Sections · Connections** (the last one per item 12). Everything in UI-002 §4 holds: tabs stay mounted, typed values are kept, **Reset** restores stored values, one command is in flight at a time, and editors appear on the head only.

### 7.1 Frame (item 4)

| Field | Input | Hint |
|---|---|---|
| Joist spacing | A length (UI-001 §7) | "Measured from each bay's west beam line. Supported: 12" to 48"." |
| Purlins per slope | A whole number | "Evenly divided between eave and ridge. Supported: 0 to 4." |
| Knee brace leg | A length | "Each leg, at 45°. Supported: 1'-6" to 4'-0", and twice the leg must fit the shortest bay while braces are on." |
| Knee braces on Level 1 | A checkbox | — |
| Knee braces at the eave | A checkbox | — |

- **Command:** `SetFrameParameters {joist_spacing_m, purlins_per_slope, knee_brace_leg_m, knee_braces_level_1, knee_braces_eave}`, all five, through the preview (consequential, D057).
- **Stored values:** an untouched length sends its stored value exactly (UI-002's `useStoredFields`), and the checkboxes send their state. Submitting without edits previews as a no-op.
- **Parsing only:** a purlin count that is not a whole number is a parse error ("Enter a whole number"). Ranges and brace fit are the server's to check (D028); the hints only state them, as the roof hints do.
- **Note:** "Ranges are what the generator supports, not structural limits. Members regenerate; members on surviving positions keep their IDs." (building-model.md §3.3, D057.)
- **Errors:** each `args.<field>` detail is placed on its field (MODEL-005 §7: each argument links to `parameters.<field>`).

### 7.2 Connection types (item 5; placement is item 12, the catalog is item 13)

One row per junction class in `FrameSystem.connection_defaults`, in the D054 table order (`post-beam`, `post-sill`, `brace`, `joist-beam`, `rafter-peak`, `rafter-foot`, `purlin-rafter`):

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Junction | The class's display label (§8.3), and D054's member description, for example "Each joist end into a beam" |
| Connections | The head snapshot's `Connection` count for the class. 0 is shown as "None in the current frame" |
| Type | A select with the class's two catalog types, each as "label (traditional)" or "label (engineered)", set to the stored type |
| — | **Preview** for that row, enabled when the select differs from the stored type |

- **Command:** `SetConnectionType {junction_class, connection_type}`, through the preview (consequential, D059).
- **Description:** under the table, the catalog description of each type chosen in a select ("… Representational only: no capacity, sizes, or fasteners.").
- **A class with no connections:** still editable (the server accepts it, D059). The row says the type applies when the frame generates connections of that class. Its preview lists no artifacts (§9).
- **Pricing note,** as in the Sections tab: "Connections are priced by type (for example `connection.PEGGED_MORTISE_TENON`). After a class changes type, the new type's line is unpriced until you price it, and the old type's row may become unused." With a **Set unit prices** link.
- **Errors:** a detail at `args.connection_type`, or one naming it in `related_paths` (for example `UNSUPPORTED_VALUE` at `connection_defaults["joist-beam"]`, CONN-001 §8), is shown on the submitted row.

### 7.3 Commands, paths, and messages (item 6)

- **Tabs of commands:** `SetFrameParameters` → Frame, `SetConnectionType` → Connections (`TAB_OF_COMMAND`), so a commit rebuilds the right tab.
- **Cross-tab pointers by path** (UI-002 §7): a `FrameSystem` path maps to a tab by its field, not by its kind alone:
  - `parameters…` → Frame;
  - `connection_defaults…` → Connections;
  - `member_defaults…` → Sections;
  - anything else on `FrameSystem` → Sections, as today.

  This resolves the limitation in MODEL-005 §14: a brace-fit error from a grid or footprint change points to the Frame tab, not the Sections tab.
- **Detail text** (`DETAIL_TEXT`): "Outside the range VS-01 supports" and "Not supported in VS-01" become "Outside the supported range" and "Not supported", because the browser supports version 2 only (D055). Codes stay visible, as today.

## 10. The catalog the editor needs (item 13, judgment)

The type select (§7.2) needs, for every class, its two allowed types, and for every type, its family and description, including types not in use. No payload or route carries this today:

- the schedule's `connection_catalog` lists only the types in use (ART-002 §6.3, approved);
- OpenAPI's enums list all five types and seven classes, but not which pairs are allowed;
- the snapshot has each class's current type only.

**Proposal: a generated catalog export.**
- The backend writes `docs/architecture/schemas/connection-catalog.json` from `model/connections.py`: the catalog version, each class's default and alternative, and each type's family and description.
- `npm run generate:api` turns it into `src/api/connectionCatalog.ts`, beside `schema.d.ts`.
- Both drift checks fail when it is stale: the backend's schema-export test and `npm run check:api`.
- There is one source, no runtime cost, and no hand copy. The cost is one more committed contract file.

**Alternatives:**
- **A hand-written client constant** mirroring D054, checked by a Vitest test against a copy that the UI fixture generator adds. Fewer build steps, but a second copy of a catalog the backend owns.
- **An API route,** for example `GET /api/v1/connection-catalog`. A runtime source, ready for per-project catalogs later, but a new route, OpenAPI entry, access rule, and fetch, for a constant that changes only with a new catalog version.
- **The full catalog in the schedule payload.** It reuses a payload, but reverses ART-002 §6.3's approved "types lists exactly the used types", needs a new generator version, and makes the editor depend on the schedule being `CURRENT`.
- **Not rejected outright, but not proposed:** offering all five types in every select and letting the server reject the disallowed pairs with `UNSUPPORTED_VALUE`. It needs no catalog, but three of every five choices would fail.

## 15. Implementation guidance (not prescriptive)

- **Order:**
  1. labels and colors (§4, §8.3);
  2. markers and grouping as a pure function with its own tests, then the plan, then 3D;
  3. selection;
  4. the Frame tab, then the connection-type rows and their catalog;
  5. schedule tables, then the dialog;
  6. the fixture captures and Playwright last.
- **Grouping:** one pure function over a plane's markers (or the mesh's), keyed by the exact coordinates, used by both views and their tests.
- **3D markers:** one `InstancedMesh` per type (or per group style) keeps 193 glyphs cheap. Render order and `depthTest: false` give item 11's proposal.
- **Selection state:** a small union in the workspace (`{kind: "member", id}` or `{kind: "connections", ids}`), with a selector that derives the highlighted member and connection IDs for each view.
- **Editors:** `FrameEditor.tsx` and `ConnectionsEditor.tsx` in `workspace/editors/`, with argument building as pure functions in `model.ts`, as UI-002 did.
